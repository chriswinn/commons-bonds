# Find the source before proposing a cut

**From:** the author, 2026-11-26, in the Chapter 5 sources session. **Scope:** every claims check and every
chapter.

**The rule.** When a sentence has no source on file, the first move is to look for one, by every route, and
to ask him for what only he can fetch. Do not offer "cut it" as the easy way out, and do not tell him an item
can be skipped because its sentence might go. He decides what to cut only after the search is done and
recorded.

**Why (his words).** "I'd like to go ahead and find everything and then make decisions once we have
everything vs. make decisions to cut things instead of searching for them as that potentially diminishes the
impact, strength & value of this book at the cost of simply finding a source."

**How to apply.** Keep one find-list per stage (first one:
`tools/audits/sources-to-find_master-list_2026-11-26.md`). A cut may still be the right ruling when a source
contradicts the sentence or the search is exhausted; say which of the two it is. *(The "how to apply" lines are
the session's reading.)*

**Translated works (author, 2026-11-26; this replaces an earlier note the same day).** Anything that rests on a
session's own translation carries a risk, so:
- A free copy in the original language is good for **checking** that a citation says what it is cited for.
  Fetch it, quote the original, and mark any translation as the session's own.
- It is **not** enough to rely on in the book. Always also request a **published English translation**
  through the library, and quote or paraphrase from that, naming the translator and edition.
- His words: "We should probably request English translations as anything that is based on a translation runs
  a risk I had not thought of."
- **Clarified by him the same day:** the risk is in "any citation that is based on a translation that you or I
  did vs. the author / publisher approved." So the rule covers his own translations as well as a session's.
  No citation, quotation or paraphrase in the book may rest on a translation made by him or by a session. It
  rests on a translation the author or publisher approved, with translator and edition named. Quoting a work
  in its original language is not affected.

- **Refined by him 2026-11-27.** His concern is an argument resting on a translation error that he or a
  session made. A **published** translation by a named translator from a reputable press meets it, and where
  such a translation is out of copyright and free, fetch that first (Kant in T. K. Abbott's translation,
  Longmans, is the example). The citation must then name the translation actually used. A library request for
  a modern edition is still worth making when the book will quote the work or when reviewers will expect the
  standard scholarly edition.
