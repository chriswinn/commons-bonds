name: The dinner-table explanation rule (the tone of the book)
description: Author, 2026-11-12. Write the book the way you would explain it to the author across a dinner table. If your explanation of a passage is clearer than the passage, the explanation is the passage. Do not compress to sound like prose. No punchy verdict closers; he doesn't talk like that.

**The rule (author, 2026-11-12):** "I like the dinner table explanation conversation rule. That feels like the
right tone to me."

**How it was found.** Walking the Ch 5 decision points, Claude offered "plain" rewrites of three passages. The
author could not follow the intent of two of them, and then pointed out that Claude's chat explanation of what
each passage meant "was plainer and easier to understand" than the rewritten prose. When Claude explains, it
writes plainly; when it "writes prose," it compresses, drops steps and reaches for a shape.

**How to apply.** For each paragraph, first answer "what is this saying?" in plain sentences, one step each,
as if the author had asked across a table. Use that as the text. Never trade a step for tightness. A short
sentence that states a fact is fine; a short verdict on what was just said ("It launders it.") comes out.

**His voice, in his words:** trained at IBM to speak in a non-regional, jargon-free way so that people anywhere
feel included; "I honestly just speak plainly these days"; the intended side effect is being easy to understand
however complicated the subject.

**Consequence:** drafts written before this date in the compressed manner (the Ch 5 rings redraft, the Ch 1
pace redraft) need a paragraph-by-paragraph "is the explanation clearer than this?" pass. Current statement:
`tools/drafting-templates/commons_bonds_house-style-sheet_v1.0.2.md`, "The first rule."
