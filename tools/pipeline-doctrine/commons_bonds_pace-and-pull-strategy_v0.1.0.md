# Pace and pull: a book-wide strategy (v0.1.0)

**SUPERSEDED by `commons_bonds_pace-and-pull-strategy_v0.1.1.md`.** **Status:** PROPOSED (Claude, 2026-11-10), from a discussion with the author. Awaiting the author's reaction.
Ch 5 is the pilot. If it holds, this goes into the drafting process before all chapters are rerun.
Companion to `tools/memory/feedback_engagement_lenses.md` (the lenses handle sentences and paragraphs;
this handles sections, chapters and the book). Guidelines, not rules or targets.

## The problem, in the author's words

The book is "an amazing first draft," and "clunky prose and pace seem like the only concrete things I can
point at and say it's not ready yet." The author wants something "pulling the reader forward to read the next
sentence, the next chapter," and a reader who arrives at the end seeing the pattern everywhere and holding a
tool to test a decision "in their personal lives, in their community, or in their country, even space colony":
is this "really the full accounting," or "a Trojan horse that will eventually cost them so much it's
essentially the end of them." Not life or death, but "pulling blinders off of the readers' eyes."

## Two problems, two fixes

- **Clunky prose is a sentence problem.** The lenses and the punctuation rule fix it: reasoning in the
  joints, the strongest thing last, no interrupted clauses, then the author's redline and a mechanical polish.
- **Pace is a structure problem.** Smooth sentences don't create pull on their own. Pull comes from what
  question is open in the reader's mind at each moment. That is decided before drafting, in the brief.

## The engine: the reader learns to see

*The Martian* runs on "will he live?" This book's engine is sight. The reader is learning to detect something
hidden, and every case is a small mystery with the same question underneath: **who is left holding the bill,
and when does it come due?** The deal looks good; the cost is already inside the walls (the author's Trojan
horse); the answer lands late. The pull is curiosity plus a growing skill: each chapter, the reader sees a
little faster and a little further. The payoff is the tool in the reader's own hands.

## Open questions at four scales

| Scale | What's open | Where it's answered |
|---|---|---|
| Sentence and paragraph | The lenses: a question before the answer, reasoning in the joints, the strongest thing last | The next sentence |
| Section | Each case opens on a person or a deal and withholds who pays | The section's end, which opens the next question (no pointers to later chapters as last lines) |
| Chapter | One question per chapter, stated or felt in the opening | The chapter's end, which hands a new question to the next chapter |
| Book | One question carried through every chapter (candidates below) | The bonds and the reader's own use of the test |

Vary the shape of each chapter's question (a mystery, a puzzle, a paradox, one person's dilemma) so the
engine never becomes a formula.

## The book's running question (author to word it)

Candidates drawn from the author's own words:
1. "Is this the full accounting?" Short enough to carry into a job offer, a town vote, a national policy or a
   colony's charter. The Trojan horse is the image for what happens when the answer is no.
2. "Before we agree to the deal, can we see who will be left holding the bill?"

## In practice

1. **A one-page question map in each chapter's brief, made before drafting:** the chapter's question (where
   it opens, where it's answered), each section's question and last line, where the one opening scene and any
   earned scene go, the relationship bridges, and how the chapter advances the book's question.
2. **A cheap book-wide pace audit** after the Ch 5 pilot: read only the first and last paragraphs of every
   chapter and section, and map which questions are open and where each one closes. It shows where pull
   breaks across the book without rereading everything.
3. **The author judges; Claude drafts to the map.** The author's tool is the reader's test at the redline (at
   the end of each paragraph, do I want to know what comes next?), not fixing pace by hand.
4. **The ending as an invitation:** the last chapter hands the test to the reader for their own decisions.
