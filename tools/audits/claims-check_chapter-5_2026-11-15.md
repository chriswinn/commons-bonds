## 1. For the author: what is not supported, worst first

Each item gives the sentence as it stands, what the sources support, and one or two sentences the sources would support. Rule on each: revise, find a source, or cut. Line numbers are in the full table.

### A. Sentences that are wrong or exposed as they stand

**1. The oil and Mexico (lines 60, 62, 148). CONTESTED.**
- Stands: "The oil did not stop at the border between the United States and Mexico." Also "The oil was the same on both sides of the border" and "on both sides of the border."
- Sources: Mexico's suit alleged the oil reached its waters. One scientist's affidavit and a 2017 paper support that. BP says all the evidence shows it did not. None of the twenty published studies found BP's oil. Two paragraphs later the chapter itself says the question "was never settled."
- Options: (a) "Mexico's government said the oil had reached its waters, and it sued BP over the damage." (b) "Whether the oil crossed into Mexican waters was disputed. Mexico's suit said it had, and BP said the evidence showed it had not." For line 62: "It was one spill. The law on the two sides of the border was different." For line 148: "In the Gulf they were out on the water."
- Also in this passage: "twenty-two scientific studies of the damage" says more than the record (the studies found no BP oil). Option: "twenty-two scientific studies of its side of the Gulf."

**2. What Darity and Mullen price (lines 246, 252). SAYS MORE THAN THE SOURCE.**
- Stands: "Their measures of the racial wealth gap, and of the gap in life expectancy, put a price on the legacy of a historical injustice." And: "Darity and Mullen price what can be measured of that legacy…: wealth gaps, gaps in life expectancy, broken transmission of knowledge, trauma passed down through generations."
- Sources: their figure is built on the wealth gap alone. The life-expectancy study in the project is a different paper (Himmelstein and eight co-authors, Darity among them, 2022). It models what reparations would do to longevity. It prices nothing, and Mullen is not an author. Nothing on file shows them pricing lost knowledge or trauma.
- Options: (a) "Their measure of the racial wealth gap puts a price on the legacy of a historical injustice, and it is the most developed backward-looking accounting in the field." (b) add, if wanted: "A 2022 study that Darity co-authored ties that wealth gap to the gap in life expectancy." For line 252: "Darity and Mullen price that legacy by the racial wealth gap, and the framework follows them."
- The McDowell sentence ("it borrows their method") then needs to say what is borrowed: measuring a legacy by a present-day gap between matched groups.

**3. Fraser's "expropriation" (lines 192, 194). SAYS MORE THAN THE SOURCE. Now checked against her own text.**
- Stands: "Fraser has two things in mind. One is the unpaid care work that keeps a society going. The other is nature." And, in the next paragraph: "They are expropriation in exactly Fraser's sense."
- Source (her 2016 article, supplied by the author and read in full): expropriation "works by confiscating capacities and resources and conscripting them into capital's circuits." What is confiscated: "labor, land, animals, tools, mineral or energy deposits—but also human beings." Its centre is "racialized dependent labor." Unpaid care work is not named; nature appears as land and deposits.
- Option: "Expropriation dispenses with the contract. In Fraser's words it works 'by confiscating capacities and resources': labor, land, mineral and energy deposits, and human beings themselves. She builds the term around the enslaved, the colonized and the dispossessed, and says most of its forms "correlate strongly with racial oppression." In her wider account capital also leans on unpaid care work and on nature without paying for either." For line 194: "They are expropriation in a sense Fraser's account makes room for."
- The last sentence of the option (care work and nature) still rests on reviews of *Cannibal Capitalism*; cut it or check the book.

**4. Social Security's figures (lines 110, 112). SAYS MORE THAN THE SOURCE: out of date, and two reports mixed.**
- Stands: "Under current law and current projections, the trust fund runs out around 2033… about 77 percent." And "roughly $22.6 trillion."
- Sources: 2033 and 77 percent are the 2025 report. $22.6 trillion is the 2024 report; the 2025 report says $25.1 trillion. A 2026 report is out. Summaries of it give 2032 and about $29.3 trillion. I could not open ssa.gov to confirm.
- Options: (a) date them from one report: "In the Trustees' 2025 report the retirement trust fund runs out in 2033… about 77 percent," and "about $25.1 trillion." (b) update all three to the 2026 report after someone reads its highlights page.

**5. Black lung (lines 254, 256). SAYS MORE THAN THE SOURCE (corrected 2026-11-15: a primary source exists).**
- Stands: "The Government Accountability Office and the Congressional Research Service put the program's payouts at roughly $44 billion through 2009." "$5.1 billion in debt as of September 2024." "The black-lung program is paid for by an excise tax on coal."
- Sources: the total is the Labor Department's, not either agency's: $44.2 billion in benefits for fiscal 1970 through 2009. It includes the program's first part, which was paid from general revenue, not the coal tax. The Treasury's statement for September 30, 2024 shows $4,969 million borrowed from the Treasury.
- Options: "The Labor Department puts the program's payouts at $44.2 billion for fiscal 1970 through 2009, the most recent official total." "Even so, the program's trust fund still owed the Treasury about $5 billion as of September 2024." "Since 1977 the program's trust fund has been paid for by an excise tax on coal."

**6. Sweden (lines 104, 106). NO SOURCE and SAYS MORE.**
- Stands: "Sweden also coordinated the rescue of its banks with changes to people's mortgages." "The scholars… treat Sweden as standing proof that a country does not have to choose." "It chose much the same way again in 2020."
- Sources: the project's notes on Kindleberger and Eichengreen credit them with the bank rescue, the 1931 exit from gold and public works. The same notes say the pairing with mortgage relief is what "the framework" adds. Neither book has been read for it. Two searches found no Swedish mortgage program. Nothing is on file for 2020.
- Options: (a) "In the 1930s Sweden rescued its banks, left the gold standard early and put people to work on public projects." (b) "Economic historians such as Barry Eichengreen credit the early exit from gold with an earlier recovery. This book reads Sweden as proof that the scope of a rescue is a choice." Cut or source the mortgage sentence and the 2020 clause.

**7. "Trillions of dollars within days" and the bonuses (lines 90, 92). SAYS MORE; NO SOURCE.**
- Stands: "The government found trillions of dollars within days for the institutions at the center of the crisis." "Within roughly eighteen months, bonuses at the rescued institutions had resumed."
- Sources: the $16 trillion is every loan added up over about two and a half years. The most outstanding at once was about $1.1 trillion to $1.6 trillion. New York's attorney general found the banks paid bonuses for 2008 itself, the year of the rescue.
- Options: "At the peak the Federal Reserve had more than a trillion dollars out on loan to those institutions, by the Government Accountability Office's audit." "The rescued banks paid billions in bonuses for 2008 itself, the year of the rescue."

**8. The herring (line 64). CONTESTED.**
- Stands: "In the years after the spill, the Pacific herring in the sound collapsed, and it never recovered," under "The same thing had happened twenty years earlier." Then: "The families… found no way to file a claim."
- Sources: the collapse came in 1993 and the stock has not recovered. The government's own 2007 review says a link to the spill "cannot be proved or disproved with certainty," and a 2011 study found no evidence of one. Nothing is on file about what herring fishermen could claim.
- Options: "Four years after the spill the sound's Pacific herring collapsed, and it has never recovered. Scientists still dispute how much of that was the oil." Cut or source the claim sentence.

**9. The spill's size and its unpaid damage (lines 52, 56). CONTESTED; NO SOURCE.**
- Stands: "Oil poured into the Gulf… roughly 4.9 million barrels of it." "…estimated at another $8 billion to $12 billion beyond what BP paid, and fifteen years after the spill those fisheries remain impaired."
- Sources: 4.9 million is the government's estimate of what left the well. The court found 3.19 million barrels reached the Gulf. The ledger already marks the $8 billion to $12 billion as having no source, and none was found today. The 2025 reports on the fisheries are mixed.
- Options: "By the government's estimate about 4.9 million barrels left the well. The court later found that about 3.2 million reached the Gulf." Cut the $8 billion to $12 billion. "Fifteen years after the spill some species had shown little or no recovery."

**10. The crowdfunding paragraph (lines 28, 30). SAYS MORE; NO SOURCE for two details.**
- Stands: the surname finding, placed beside Kenworthy and Igra; "Canada, the United Kingdom and Germany"; "Americans rely on it far more heavily, and… use it in place of insurance coverage"; the chief executive's statement.
- Sources: Saleh and colleagues, which the project holds and which was read today, found that "Black individuals raised 11.5% less… than non-Black individuals." That supports a race gap in what campaigns raise. It does not cover surnames, Hispanic organizers or reaching goals; the surname finding is in Panjwani and Xiong (2023), now on the tentative list and unread. Kenworthy and Igra do not study race. Saleh compared Canada, the UK and the US only, and found American campaigns far more often sought routine care (77.9 percent, against 21.9 and 26.6). The statement is Rob Solomon's, the former chief executive: "We didn't build the platform to focus on medical expenses."
- Options: (a) "A 2020 study of campaigns in three countries found that Black beneficiaries raised about 11 percent less than others." (b) keep the surname sentence, after Panjwani and Xiong is read and named in it. Drop Germany. "The same study found that American campaigns asked for help with routine care far more often than Canadian or British ones." "The man who ran the platform until 2020 said it was not built for medical expenses."

**11. Christophers (line 198). SAYS MORE THAN THE SOURCE. Now checked against his own words.**
- Stands: "His work traces how a financialized economy puts the wrong price on whatever it turns into a tradable asset."
- Source (his 2022 article, supplied by the author): "investment decisions are not determined by price. The nub of investment is profit." The chapter describes an argument he does not make.
- Option: "Brett Christophers argues that what decides investment is profit, not price, so getting a price right does not by itself change what gets built." The sentences around it ("The most forceful living version of this worry comes from…") then need a second look: his argument is a reason to doubt that pricing is enough, which is a different worry from pricing turning a commons into an asset.

**12. Libby: scenes and documents with no source (lines 32, 38, 40, 42, 46, 210). NO SOURCE.**
- Stands: "onto their children's laps"; "oxygen tanks rode in grocery carts beside the bread"; "Funerals for the relatively young were not unusual"; internal documents "show lobbying and an effort to delay the regulation of asbestos"; the criminal case "failed for a different reason"; the $100 million is "an industry estimate."
- Sources: none for any of these. The grocery-cart line was written by a drafting session, and nothing found today supports it. The running track is an allegation from the indictment. The origin of the $100 million has never been found.
- Options: cut the grocery-cart and funeral sentences, or replace them with a sourced one: "By 2011, 694 people there had died of asbestos-related disease." Cut the lobbying sentence. For the acquittal: "One way to read the verdict is this." For the revenue: "No primary source for that figure has been found."

**13. "The complexity was no accident" (line 84). NO SOURCE.**
- Stands: "The complexity was no accident. Only an instrument that was too complicated to evaluate could be sold in large volume…" and "Some of those instruments ended up in the same retirement accounts."
- Sources: none on file for intent. A search of the Commission's conclusions found no such finding. The retirement-accounts sentence traces to an unsourced case file.
