## Structure

- **Who bears the cost** is now in the definition of the bond: cost counts as bonded only if it was priced with
  the cost in view. Cost severance is what was borne but never priced.
- **Cost Severance Damages and the Restitution Bond are formally defined**, with a split between what the
  extractor paid and what the public paid.
- **The Hotelling Identity is restated** as a split of the forward gap: rent that was not bonded, plus the gap
  that would remain even if it all were. Worked for Norway, where the first part is about zero.
- **"Three Ways of Counting" is kept and glossed:** two ways estimate what is owed, one reads what was paid.
  All language about triangulation and convergence is gone.
- **Gate 1 is one three-step test.** The appendix says which gates are substantive and that the gates do not
  test causation.
- **New:** a table of what is borrowed and what is added; a section on the closest prior art (assurance bonds,
  natural resource damage assessment, true cost accounting, user cost, the coal damage literature); one
  thought-experiment showing a reserve sized in advance; one worked backward case (the Chesapeake oyster reef).

## Passages on his ground

- The method for pricing realized harm is stated as **Darity and Mullen's**. The framework adopts it and adds
  nothing to it.
- The Restitution Bond is named from **their published definition**: redress is the act of restitution. General
  public programs are no longer listed as restitution.
- **Nothing from the May 2026 conversation is in the appendix.** The "(Darity 2026)" citations and the interview
  entry in his copy are removed. Ostrom is positioned on her own published principles.
- The coercion variable remains named and unpriced.
