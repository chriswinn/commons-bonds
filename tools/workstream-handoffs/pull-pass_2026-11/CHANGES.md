## What this pass taught about drafting for pull

1. **The pull came from structure, not sentences.** Most of the base's sentences survived. What changed is
   which question is open when a reader reaches each one. The question map took one page and decided more
   than any line edit did.
2. **"Name it later" means the naming needs a moment to arrive at.** Once the pocket existed as a climax,
   "severed cost" and the author's pattern lines had an obvious home. Before that, there was nowhere for them
   to go but the front.
3. **Process notes cluster at the joints.** Nearly every announced hedge sat at a section turn or before a
   number. That's where the writer is nervous about the next step, so those are the joints to read with the
   end-of-paragraph test.
4. **Relationships make better transitions than scale words.** "A family that cannot pay asks the people who
   know it" carried more than "step back again," because it hands the reader a person rather than a camera
   move.
5. **The cost is the back half.** Rings put the climax at about 60 percent of the chapter, and the argument
   sections now follow it. That's the next pace problem (critic read, item 1). The fix is probably
   allocation across chapters, not sentences.
