# Strongest-critic read of draft B (run once)

Where a hostile but fair reader would stop, as questions for the author. Seven.

1. **Does the chapter still prove what its title says?** By ¶21 the reader learns that Norway's unpaid cost is
   about as large as its famous fund. A hostile reader says: then Norway kept the money and exported the bill,
   and that is the pattern the book condemns, done politely. ¶23 answers ("what Norway proves is narrower"), but
   it comes fifteen paragraphs after a child is born holding $390,000. Do you want the limit of the proof
   stated near the top, or is it stronger arriving where it does?
2. **The carbon floor counts every barrel as burned, three paragraphs after the chapter says some barrels become
   surgical trays and asphalt.** 0.37 tons × 55 billion assumes combustion. Is that the ledger's intent (a floor
   on the whole shelf), and should the prose say "if burned" or give the share that is burned?
3. **One contested number carries the $1.9 trillion.** $190 a ton is one United States agency's 2023 estimate,
   used without a range, on Norwegian oil burned worldwide. The ledger's own sensitivity for coal shows the
   answer moves a great deal at $51. Should the chapter say in a clause that the figure is one agency's and that
   a lower one shrinks the bill but does not erase it (if that is true for Norway: at $51 the carbon is about
   $19 a barrel, below the $36 fund)?
4. **"About $36 held against $32 to $36 of rent" compares a 2025 fund balance, with thirty years of investment
   returns in it, to rent earned over fifty years.** An economist will ask what year's money each is in. And
   "70 to 80 cents of every dollar" has been flagged since June as mixing a marginal tax rate with a total
   share. Keep both as they are, soften, or drop the 70-to-80 sentence?
5. **"What reached the communities that bore the costs rounds to zero."** A reader who knows Nigeria knows that
   oil-producing states receive a constitutional share of revenue and that a development commission exists for
   the Delta. The chapter's point may be that the money did not reach the people. Does the sentence need the
   record behind it, or a narrower wording?
6. **Alaska, Chattanooga, Mondragón and Vienna.** Only Alaska is about a resource. A hostile reader says the
   chapter changes its subject from "who pays for extraction" to "collective ownership works," and that Alaska
   pays its fund out as dividends each year, which is closer to spending the windfall than to Norway. Do all four
   stay here at full length, or does Alaska stay and the other three shrink or wait for Chapter 9?
7. **Whose choice was it?** "Cost severance is a policy choice" follows a paragraph saying Nigeria's terms were
   written before it was a country, by others. A fair reader asks who chose. The drafts say "people engineered
   both," without saying which people in Nigeria's case. And ¶26 says the McDowell companies "were not unusually
   malicious": does that match what Chapter 2's record shows, under your rule that a documented act is named
   plainly?
