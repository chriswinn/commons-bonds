# Chapter 10, draft B: one strongest-critic read (2026-11-14)

**Status:** PROPOSED (internal). Run once, on the pace draft. These are the places a hostile but fair reader would stop, put as questions for the author. Nothing here has been changed in the drafts.

1. **Is social distance a real fourth kind, or is it there to match the appendix?** It has one example (your colleague in Chapter 1), and no instrument is said to close it, while the other three each have a case and an instrument. Do you have a stronger case for it, and is there an instrument that closes any of it?

2. **Was 2008 really a matter of information "above all"?** Chapter 5 tells 2008 as a choice about who was rescued, which this section would call power. A critic will say the chapter files its cases under whichever kind is convenient. Do you want 2008 under information, under power, or under both?

3. **"It was that system operating as it was designed to operate."** This is a claim about design, and the sentence cites nothing. The rule elsewhere in the book is to allege no intent the record does not show. What record do you want cited here (the 1877 Act itself, the Court's 1980 opinion, Dunbar-Ortiz), or do you want the sentence to claim less?

4. **Five writers are named and none is quoted.** The passage tells the reader to go and read Kimmerer, Simpson, Coulthard, Dunbar-Ortiz and Deloria, and says their question is deeper, without saying what any one of them argues. A critic will call that a list of names in place of engagement. Do you want one sentence on what one of them says, or is pointing the reader to them the whole of what you mean to do?

5. **"The framework legislates nothing," from a book named for an instrument.** A conservative critic will answer that accountability bonds and reserves exist only if a state requires them, so the book does take a side. A critic on the left will say the same from the other direction. Is the honest claim that the measurement takes no side and the instruments in Chapter 9 are your proposals?

6. **"The first generation… very likely the last with meaningful leverage."** Nothing supports this in the chapter: no figure, no source, and "several of them are in this decade" names none. Which decision points do you mean, and do you want Gardiner credited here in a clause?

7. **The shrunk middle objection names four thinkers in six sentences and leans on two other chapters.** A reader who has forgotten Chapter 5 gets Polanyi, Pistor, Christophers and Sandel at speed, and "Chapter 9 answered Christophers" depends on a chapter not yet rebuilt. Is two paragraphs the right size, or does this chapter need a few more sentences of the answer so it can be read without the others?
