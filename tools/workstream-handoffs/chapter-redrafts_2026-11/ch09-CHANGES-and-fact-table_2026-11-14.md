## 3. Things in the drafts that are new and need the author's eye

1. **Sweden in 1992 is told differently from the old chapter.** The old chapter said the 1992 rescue included
   household protection. The sources I read today do not say that (the Cleveland Fed commentary says the government
   withdrew a consumer-debt subsidy). What they do support: creditors guaranteed, shareholders not; the state took
   ownership of Nordbanken and Gota; bad loans to Securum and Retriva; leftover returned to the treasury. The drafts
   say that, and read it as the bond's logic applied after the fact. That reading is mine.
2. **Section 6 states five features of the bond** (who posts, sized to what, held for whom, released when, why it
   can't be sold). The brief asked for exactly those five. The ledger defines the bond and the payer split; it does
   not say who holds the money or when it is released, and I did not load the Technical Appendix. "Held in a fund
   they will inherit" and "released when the cost has been made good" are my plain statements of the idea and must
   be checked against TA Definitions 1.7, 1.11 and 1.12 before promotion.
3. **The close has the author's five figures, then one more person,** "a person with a vote on the seabed who has
   not yet cast it." The map says five. Strike the sentence if five is the rule.
4. **Two facts that were in the old chapter are now corrected:** carbon pricing (old: "more than seventy
   jurisdictions… a quarter of emissions"; now the World Bank's 2026 count); Alaska (old: about $80 billion; now
   Ch 4's figure).
5. **Insertion E's wording.** "Permanent foreclosure" is written as the proviso's plainest case, followed by "the
   same test applies to a living stock taken faster than it renews," so it does not read as a limit on scope.
