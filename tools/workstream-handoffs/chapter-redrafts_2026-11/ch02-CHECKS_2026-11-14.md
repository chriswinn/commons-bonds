## 6. One strongest-critic read of Draft B (run once; questions for the author)

A hostile but fair reader stops in these places.

1. **"Saints in the boardroom," then guilty pleas.** The chapter says no villain is needed, then gives two felony pleas. A critic asks which it is. Does the one added sentence do enough, or does the "no villain" paragraph need to say outright that it describes the coal and not every case?
2. **The scrip passage argues against itself.** It gives Athey's reading, then Fishback's finding that prices were similar and miners were generally not in debt, then draws a conclusion no source measures. Is that honest balance, or does it leave the reader unsure why the passage is there? Would it be stronger as description only, ending at "the snake"?
3. **The $44 billion is seventeen years old.** A reader checking the black lung column finds a figure "through 2009" in a book published far later. Is the dated floor enough, or should the chapter wait for a current total?
4. **The children.** "The county spent itself raising the very people who had to leave it to live" treats moving away as a pure loss. An economist replies that the people who left were better off, and that migration is how workers escape a declining place. Does the chapter need a sentence granting that and saying the cost fell on the place and the people who stayed?
5. **The two opioid records.** The chapter says no document "confesses to a plan" and then says the drug "found" the collapsed places. A critic asks what the evidence is, since no study is named in the text. Should one source be named in the sentence (the citation spec lists Van Zee 2009)?
6. **"I am coining them here."** A critic who knows "value extraction" from Mazzucato will say the term is not new. The sentence before credits her for the distinction. Is "coining" right for all three terms, or only for "cost severance" and "severed cost"?
7. **The bay arrives late and fast.** One page from the end the chapter moves to the Chesapeake and the author's own childhood. Does a reader who has spent the whole chapter in McDowell feel that as a turn toward the next chapter, or as a second ending?
