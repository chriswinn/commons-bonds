Internal. One page. Content source: `manuscript/chapters/Chapter__3_TheWaterman.md` (promoted 2026-06-11; four marked additions from 2026-06-17; not yet author-redlined). The content is settled. The prose is not: redraft it fresh for pace, pull and flow against the style sheet, and do not match the current wording. The largest pace problem is repetition: the same comparison and the same figures are stated several times, and a reader meets five fisheries in a row before meeting another person. None of the calls below is a length target.

**The chapter's question.** A reef makes more of itself every year if you leave it the means. So how did a renewable thing get used up like a coal seam, and who is paying to put it back? Opened by the shell driveway ("the material a reef is rebuilt from, spread under a family's cars"). Answered in the reef column: the public is paying, and the industry's line is zero.

**Book questions advanced.** "Who is left holding the bill?" The watermen, then the public. "Is this the full accounting?" No, and the chapter says so: it prices only the reef bottom and leaves the rest open on purpose.

**Terms handed over.** "Restitution Bond," named and run once, with the building of it left for later. The difference between finite and renewable drawdown (Ch 2 closed on it).

**Opening person and place.** The harbor at Old Point Comfort before dawn. Earned scenes: Biggie and his six-pack of Coca-Cola; the net-mending morning. Earned return: the same harbor at the close, with Biggie's lunch "waiting for him in the water."
