**Title and gloss (author, 2026-11-11).** "Three Ways of Counting" stays. Two ways count what is owed; one counts what was paid. Counting what was paid is half of balancing the books, and the difference between the two sides is the accountability gap. No "triangulation," no "convergence."

**The chapter's question.** The earlier chapters showed that a bill goes unpaid. How large is it, and how would anyone know the number wasn't simply picked? Answered in three counts and one subtraction: what is owed, checked two ways; what was paid, and by whom; the gap between them.

**Book questions advanced.** "Who is left holding the bill?" gets its first measured answer: where a bill is being paid at all, the public pays most of it. "Is this the full accounting?" The chapter says what it leaves unpriced and why (the Open state).

**Terms handed over.** "Residual commons value" (what is owed), "accountability bond" (what was posted), and the gap between them; the three states of an entry (Zero, Filled, Open); the Commons Inversion Test. The Asymmetric Regret Rule in a paragraph, for Ch 7 and Ch 9.

**Opening person and place (see call 1).** Prince William Sound in 1989 and the families who fished its herring. Ch 5 hands this case to Ch 6 for "the full accounting." One spill, three different bills: what the company paid, what the cleanup cost, and what a fishery that never came back was worth. Which one is "the cost"?
