## My pick: Roadmap first (the house)

**Ranking:** 1. Roadmap first · 2. Earned roadmap · 3. Out and back.

### Why Roadmap first

1. **Your sentence is the first line, and the next sentence turns it into real places.** The
   roadmap is a list of concrete places (a mining town, an oil rig, a rare-earth plant, a
   retirement account, a pay stub, a hospital room), not a promise to "walk through" anything.
2. **It has the strongest structural turn of any version.** "So far, everything in this house has
   put you on the receiving end of the value… The mailbox is where you start paying." The reader
   goes from beneficiary to payer at exactly the moment your 2008 beat arrives. The no-alibi point
   is built into the structure instead of being announced.
3. **It implicates the reader without accusing.** The no-villain line comes in the fifth paragraph,
   before any case. That serves your Goal 2 (people seeing their own position with open eyes) more
   directly than the other two.
4. **It has the most memorable close.** "The house you live in is a ledger, though no one keeps it"
   and "The house will also outlast you" tie the Foreclosure Bond to an image the reader is sitting
   inside.
5. **It passes Darity's test most plainly.** Every case enters through an object he can picture. The
   argument sections are identical across versions, so this is purely about how well the cases carry
   the idea.

### Its risks

- **Second person all the way through can feel presumptuous.** Not every reader has an attic, a car
  or a 401(k).
- **A few links are thin.** The "same tank" connection to Valdez is the weakest; the Gulf-oil link to
  the car rests on "some of that oil."
- **To an academic reader the device could read as a device.** Redline lever: soften a few "you"
  lines and cut the Valdez tank link if it bothers you.

## Why Earned roadmap is second

It has the best single idea in the round. The close says the unborn are "farther away than Baotou,
in time rather than space." The escalation also recovers the old chapter's strongest feature, the
climb to the dying body, with a stated principle instead of "scatter."

But it **opens on Baotou, the chapter's least-documented case**; the chapter itself says the
evidence there is limited. Its first page is processing chemistry: acids, tons of wastewater,
thorium. That is a credibility and engagement risk on page one, especially with Darity.

## Why Out and back is third

The structure is elegant, and the return to the room is the most moving ending. But its **first page
is a reported account of Medicare payment figures from someone else's family**, and the household
section right after is the densest run of statistics in any version. It is the most conceptual start
and the least gripping.

## Self-critique that applies to all three

- **Average sentence length is 14 words, shorter than your own Ch 1 (18).** Some passages may read
  clipped. Your redline can lengthen sentences where the rhythm wants it; the rule only forbids
  splitting a clause with an insertion, not long sentences as such.
- **The case paragraphs are deliberately the same across versions,** so the differences are the
  opening, the order and the framing. If one version wins on content alone, that's a sign the
  difference is structural rather than about prose quality.
