### 1c. The structure question — does scale-as-spine (A) beat scatter-as-spine (B/C)?

**Yes, on the merits, with one taste-dependent caveat.**

- **For A:** the chapter's load-bearing meta-claim is "define a commons by its structure and the reef
  and the atmosphere and the body of labor and the unborn collapse into one object." In B/C that claim
  is *asserted* in a single (excellent) passage near the end. In A the reader has *already felt it*,
  because the camera showed the identical mechanism at eight magnifications before the chapter ever
  names it; the "same object, named honestly" section lands as recognition, not assertion. A also
  gives the two instruments a structural home (Restitution = the near rings already passed; Foreclosure
  = the furthest ring, the unborn), which B/C name without that spatial logic.
- **Against A (taste, not defect):** (1) the camera conceit is explicit and sustained ("pull the
  camera back one notch," "the camera turns around"); a reader who finds it gimmicky loses gravity.
  (2) Out-and-back opens on the dying body, so the chapter's current *escalation* to the dying body as
  the final, most intimate devastation is traded for a bookend. C/B earn a slow climb to Butler's room
  as the last word; A spends that card on page one and buys a structural payoff instead. This is a real
  aesthetic trade and it is exactly the kind of call only the author can make.
