## My pick: Roadmap first (the house), narrowly over Earned roadmap

**Ranking:** 1. Roadmap first · 2. Earned roadmap (close behind) · 3. Out and back.

### Why Roadmap first

1. **Its structural turn is the strongest in the round.** "Everything in the house so far has put you
   on the receiving end of the value… The mailbox is where you start paying." The reader flips from
   beneficiary to payer exactly where your 2008 account begins, so the no-alibi point is built into
   the structure.
2. **It has the best single lines.** "Its cost is still being paid in Libby, in the lungs of people
   who will never see the inside of your house," and the close: "The house you live in is a ledger,
   even though no one keeps it."
3. **Your sentence opens it,** and every case arrives through an object the reader can picture,
   which is the plainest test of whether the prose carries the idea.

### Its risks

- **The first paragraph is heavy.** The roadmap sentence runs to 55 words, a long list before the
  reader has a reason to care. A redline lever: end it after "a hospital room" and give the house its
  own short sentence.
- **Second person all the way through** can feel presumptuous to readers without an attic, a car or a
  401(k).
- **The car-to-Gulf link is the thinnest** ("some of that oil").

## Why Earned roadmap is a close second

In round 3 it is the most humane of the three, because the cases are told from the side of the people
paying. "The people of Libby paid for that success with their lungs." The roadmap is earned and
clear, and its close has the round's best idea: the unborn are "farther away than Baotou, in time
rather than space." It is also the closest of the three to your own person-to-group instinct.

**What holds it back** is that it opens on Baotou, the chapter's least-documented case, with a first
paragraph of processing chemistry (acids, tons of wastewater, thorium). That is a risk with a careful
first reader like Darity.

**What would flip my pick:** if that opening scene works for you on paper, I'd rank Earned roadmap
first.

## Why Out and back is third

The structure is the most elegant, and the return to the room is moving. But it opens with a
reported sentence ("the father of the journalist Katy Butler was given a pacemaker") and goes
straight into Medicare payment figures, so the first page asks for attention before it earns it. The
household section that follows is the densest run of statistics in any version.

## Notes on all three

- **No length target this time.** The average sentence came out around 21 words without any length
  target. All three have zero clauses split by an insertion.
- **Some passages are deliberately shared:** your 2008 account, the 2008 objections, the Social
  Security argument and the closing argument sections. The differences are in the openings, the case
  narratives and the structure.
