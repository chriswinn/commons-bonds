# Ch 5 round 3 — what works and what doesn't, by version (Claude, 2026-11-09)

**Status:** internal notes for the pull pass on **Out and back** (the author's pick). Written after the
author's pick so the next session doesn't have to re-derive them. The author's responses go in the last
section. These are observations to weigh during drafting, not rules.

## Out and back (the pick)

**Works**
- **The frame.** Opening in the room raises a question that the return to the room answers. It also gives
  the argument sections a direction: they sit on "the way back in" ("Coming back in from that distance,
  toward the room where the chapter began"), so the back half has motion the other two versions lack.
- **The close.** "A pacemaker kept a heart beating through years of decline, and at seventy-seven a woman
  kept watch more than eighty hours a week while no one kept a ledger of it."
- **Openers with a question or a stake in them:** "At the scale of a whole national economy, the alibi
  fails"; Time's "the sharpest version of the mechanism hides inside the system Americans think of as the
  opposite of extraction"; the town's Zonolite bridge ("some of the town's product is above your head right
  now").
- **A list that builds, framed:** "From the farthest point every scale is in view at once: a body, a
  household, a town, a coast, a supply chain, a nation, a lifetime of promises and the unborn."
- **A promise that pulls:** the unborn section ends "On the way back in, the chapter will name an instrument
  for each."

**Doesn't**
- **"Step back" five times** (end of One body, town, coast, supply chain, time). The scale motion is the
  spine, but the same verb at every door becomes the formula the author warned about.
- **The same opener template:** "Step back again, and what is being drawn on is…" (town, coast) puts the
  mechanism's vocabulary ahead of the people.
- **Page two drops.** Straight after the opening scene, "A household" runs ~500 words of crowdfunding
  statistics and citations with no person in it ("far more" twice in two sentences, too). That's where the
  author's first-page pull is most at risk.
- **Baotou** opens by removing the people ("drop out of sight entirely"); see lens 8.
- **Process notes:** the "rendered strictly within what she wrote" sentence in the opening.
- **The town ends on Pistor** ("The work of this book is to price what those modules sever") rather than on
  Libby.

## Earned roadmap

**Works**
- The Baotou opening scene and its bridge ("within arm's reach of you right now").
- Transitions that promise stakes: "It comes out of your own paycheck, and in time it will come out of your
  children's."
- The most humane telling: the cases are told from the side of the people paying ("who was left holding the
  loss").
- "Farther away than Baotou, in time rather than space."

**Doesn't**
- The roadmap paragraph is an unframed catalogue.
- The promise is announced three times ("come closer to you with every step"; "Every case after this one is
  closer"; "the closest of all"), and "closer" at every door is the same formula risk as "step back."
- The most personal case (Butler) arrives last, after a page of statistics, and opens on "the mechanism."
- It opens on the chapter's least-documented case.

## Roadmap first

**Works**
- Every case enters through an object the reader can picture, often with the reader doing something:
  "Climb into the attic of a house…"
- The turn: "Everything in the house so far has put you on the receiving end of the value… The mailbox is
  where you start paying."
- The best single lines: "its cost is still being paid in Libby, in the lungs of people who will never see
  the inside of your house"; "The house you live in is a ledger, even though no one keeps it."

**Doesn't**
- The opening list is an unframed catalogue ending on a hedge (the author's diagnosis).
- Counting signposts ("the third place in the house where you may be the one paying").
- The car-to-Gulf link is thin ("some of that oil"), and second person throughout can presume.

## Shared by all three (the argument sections, 2008 and Social Security)

- **Four sections end on a pointer to a later chapter** (coast, "The same object," "Who shaped the
  system?", restitution: "Chapter 6 gives Valdez…", "a later chapter takes up…", "Chapter 9 develops…",
  "it belongs to Chapter 9…"). Each spends the section's last position on a deferral (lens 3). Move the
  pointer earlier or into a note and end on the strongest line, the way the nation section already does
  ("What matters here is that the road taken was chosen, not forced"). For the coast, that line is "the
  season has stayed gone."
- **"Who shaped the system?" clears its throat** ("So far the framework has left one question to the side,
  and it is worth bringing back on purpose…").
- **The longest stretches** are the nation (~2,200 words) and restitution (~2,200). That's not a length
  problem but a place to apply the reader's test paragraph by paragraph.

## Candidate borrowings into Out and back

  space."
- From Roadmap first: the Libby closing line, as the town's last sentence in place of Pistor.

## Author's responses

- **Mailbox (2026-11-09):** "The mailbox is where you start paying" was clever but feels dated; the author
  has paid every bill and received every pay stub online, paperless, for years. **Lesson for bridges:** the
  object has to be in the reader's life now, or the bridge breaks and the line reads as a device. Out and
  back has no paper-era props (checked). If the pull pass adds a bridge for Social Security, tie it to the
  deduction from every paycheck, not to a paper stub or a statement in the mail.
- **Butler opening (2026-11-09):** agreed the sourcing note must go: "It takes a heartwrenching story and
  makes it completely forgettable and reduces it until it's hard to follow and doesn't really have any
  meaningful impact." **So the opening needs a real revision, not a one-sentence cut.** Claude's reading of
  why: the paragraph keeps Butler's *mechanism* (payment figures, liability) and drops her *story*. The
  father is the object of a passive verb ("was given a pacemaker"), the sourcing note breaks in at
  sentence two, an abstraction follows ("a single body sitting inside a system of payment"), and the
  numbers arrive before the reader knows why they matter. The mother's eighty-hour weeks come only in
  paragraph two. **Origin:** the sourcing note leaked from an earlier drafting instruction into the current
  chapter (`Chapter__5_TheAccountabilityGap.md`, the Butler paragraph) and every round inherited it. Briefs
  should keep sourcing constraints in a block marked for the drafter only.
- **Fact flag found while checking (2026-11-09):** the drafts say Butler's mother was **seventy-seven**
  (carried from the current chapter); `research/case-studies/healthcare-end-of-life.md` says **80**. Neither
  age is in the canonical ledger, so round 3's 35/35 check did not cover it. The **$54** figure is supported
  by Butler's related published essay; **$461**, **~$12,000** and the mother's age still need checking against
  her *New York Times Magazine* essay "What Broke My Father's Heart" (June 2010) or *Knocking on Heaven's
  Door* (2013). Verify before the author's redline; the age also appears in the chapter's closing section.
