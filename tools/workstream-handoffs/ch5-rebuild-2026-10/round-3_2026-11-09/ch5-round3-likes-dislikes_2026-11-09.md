# Ch 5 round 3 — what works and what doesn't, by version (Claude, 2026-11-09)

**Status:** internal notes for the pull pass on **Out and back** (the author's pick). Written after the
author's pick so the next session doesn't have to re-derive them. The author's responses go in the last
section. These are observations to weigh during drafting, not rules.

## Out and back (the pick)

**Works**
- **The frame.** Opening in the room raises a question that the return to the room answers. It also gives
  the argument sections a direction: they sit on "the way back in" ("Coming back in from that distance,
  toward the room where the chapter began"), so the back half has motion the other two versions lack.
- **The close.** "A pacemaker kept a heart beating through years of decline, and at seventy-seven a woman
  kept watch more than eighty hours a week while no one kept a ledger of it."
- **Openers with a question or a stake in them:** "At the scale of a whole national economy, the alibi
  fails"; Time's "the sharpest version of the mechanism hides inside the system Americans think of as the
  opposite of extraction"; the town's Zonolite bridge ("some of the town's product is above your head right
  now").
- **A list that builds, framed:** "From the farthest point every scale is in view at once: a body, a
  household, a town, a coast, a supply chain, a nation, a lifetime of promises and the unborn."
- **A promise that pulls:** the unborn section ends "On the way back in, the chapter will name an instrument
  for each."

**Doesn't**
- **"Step back" five times** (end of One body, town, coast, supply chain, time). The scale motion is the
  spine, but the same verb at every door becomes the formula the author warned about.
- **The same opener template:** "Step back again, and what is being drawn on is…" (town, coast) puts the
  mechanism's vocabulary ahead of the people.
- **Page two drops.** Straight after the opening scene, "A household" runs ~500 words of crowdfunding
  statistics and citations with no person in it ("far more" twice in two sentences, too). That's where the
  author's first-page pull is most at risk.
- **Baotou** opens by removing the people ("drop out of sight entirely"); see lens 8.
- **Process notes:** the "rendered strictly within what she wrote" sentence in the opening.
- **The town ends on Pistor** ("The work of this book is to price what those modules sever") rather than on
  Libby.

## Earned roadmap

**Works**
- The Baotou opening scene and its bridge ("within arm's reach of you right now").
- Transitions that promise stakes: "It comes out of your own paycheck, and in time it will come out of your
  children's."
- The most humane telling: the cases are told from the side of the people paying ("who was left holding the
  loss").
- "Farther away than Baotou, in time rather than space."

**Doesn't**
- The roadmap paragraph is an unframed catalogue.
- The promise is announced three times ("come closer to you with every step"; "Every case after this one is
  closer"; "the closest of all"), and "closer" at every door is the same formula risk as "step back."
- The most personal case (Butler) arrives last, after a page of statistics, and opens on "the mechanism."
- It opens on the chapter's least-documented case.

## Roadmap first

**Works**
- Every case enters through an object the reader can picture, often with the reader doing something:
  "Climb into the attic of a house…"
- The turn: "Everything in the house so far has put you on the receiving end of the value… The mailbox is
  where you start paying."
- The best single lines: "its cost is still being paid in Libby, in the lungs of people who will never see
  the inside of your house"; "The house you live in is a ledger, even though no one keeps it."

**Doesn't**
- The opening list is an unframed catalogue ending on a hedge (the author's diagnosis).
- Counting signposts ("the third place in the house where you may be the one paying").
- The car-to-Gulf link is thin ("some of that oil"), and second person throughout can presume.

## Shared by all three (the argument sections, 2008 and Social Security)

- **Four sections end on a pointer to a later chapter** (coast, "The same object," "Who shaped the
  system?", restitution: "Chapter 6 gives Valdez…", "a later chapter takes up…", "Chapter 9 develops…",
  "it belongs to Chapter 9…"). Each spends the section's last position on a deferral (lens 3). Move the
  pointer earlier or into a note and end on the strongest line, the way the nation section already does
  ("What matters here is that the road taken was chosen, not forced"). For the coast, that line is "the
  season has stayed gone."
- **"Who shaped the system?" clears its throat** ("So far the framework has left one question to the side,
  and it is worth bringing back on purpose…").
- **The longest stretches** are the nation (~2,200 words) and restitution (~2,200). That's not a length
  problem but a place to apply the reader's test paragraph by paragraph.

## Author's responses

- **Mailbox (2026-11-09):** "The mailbox is where you start paying" was clever but feels dated; the author
  has paid every bill and received every pay stub online, paperless, for years. **Lesson for bridges:** the
  object has to be in the reader's life now, or the bridge breaks and the line reads as a device. Out and
  back has no paper-era props (checked). If the pull pass adds a bridge for Social Security, tie it to the
  deduction from every paycheck, not to a paper stub or a statement in the mail.
- **Butler opening (2026-11-09):** agreed the sourcing note must go: "It takes a heartwrenching story and
  makes it completely forgettable and reduces it until it's hard to follow and doesn't really have any
  meaningful impact." **So the opening needs a real revision, not a one-sentence cut.** Claude's reading of
  why: the paragraph keeps Butler's *mechanism* (payment figures, liability) and drops her *story*. The
  father is the object of a passive verb ("was given a pacemaker"), the sourcing note breaks in at
  sentence two, an abstraction follows ("a single body sitting inside a system of payment"), and the
  numbers arrive before the reader knows why they matter. The mother's eighty-hour weeks come only in
  paragraph two. **Origin:** the sourcing note leaked from an earlier drafting instruction into the current
  chapter (`Chapter__5_TheAccountabilityGap.md`, the Butler paragraph) and every round inherited it. Briefs
  should keep sourcing constraints in a block marked for the drafter only.
- **Fact flag found while checking (2026-11-09):** the drafts say Butler's mother was **seventy-seven**
  (carried from the current chapter); `research/case-studies/healthcare-end-of-life.md` says **80**. Neither
  age is in the canonical ledger, so round 3's 35/35 check did not cover it. The **$54** figure is supported
  by Butler's related published essay; **$461**, **~$12,000** and the mother's age still need checking against
  her *New York Times Magazine* essay "What Broke My Father's Heart" (June 2010) or *Knocking on Heaven's
  Door* (2013). Verify before the author's redline; the age also appears in the chapter's closing section.
- **Relationships before objects (2026-11-09):** "how many of the target readers are going to have a typical
  house, with an attic, mailbox, physical paystub, etc.? … as long as humans exist we will always have
  parents, loved ones, and a community. Regardless if we are here or have moved out into the stars." Added
  to lens 8. **For the pull pass:** bridge through relationships where the substrate already carries them.
  Butler: a daughter, her father and a wife at his side, in a room most families sit in sooner or later.
  Social Security: parents' benefits and children's taxes. Baotou: the villagers report birth defects, so
  their children are the stake. Keep the cellphone only as Baotou's literal link to the reader. This also
  explains part of the pick: Out and back's frame is a family, while Roadmap first's frame was a house.
- **"Cellphone" (2026-11-09):** agreed the phone stays only as Baotou's literal link to the reader, but call
  it a **cellphone**: most people in developed countries have one at this point in history. Keep the hedge
  ("may well be"); the claim is about where the elements may have been refined, not about every device.

## Claude's proposed defaults for Session 2 (2026-11-10; pending the author's OK)

The author agrees "Step back" could be smoother but would need time to find a fix, so these are offered as
defaults that Session 2 drafts and the author judges on paper.

1. **"Step back" (revised 2026-11-10 after the author's question):** the repeated zoom is not the problem,
   and Claude's earlier "keep it twice" default is withdrawn. A refrain works when it is a deliberate signal
   and each step lands somewhere new (the Eames film *Powers of Ten* is the model: the same motion at every
   step, gripping because every landing surprises). What read as a tic was the sentence after the verb: "Step
   back again, and what is being drawn on is…" announces the mechanism instead of showing what is new at
   that scale. Keep the refrain if it serves the spine; make each landing a person, a place or a surprise.
2. **Page-two drop:** open "A household" on the relationship (a family that can't pay asks the people who
   know it, which is what a fundraising page is), then the outcomes as the punch (nearly nine in ten fell
   short; about one in six got nothing). Lighten the run of citations.
3. **Town:** the attic bridge is an object (the author's house concern), and the section ends on Pistor. The
   draft already holds a relationship bridge in its facts: miners "carried the dust home on their clothes,
   into their kitchens and onto their children's laps." Lead with the people and end on Libby's people;
   Roadmap first's "lungs" line can be adapted without the house.
4. **Section endings:** move the four pointers to later chapters earlier in the paragraph or into a note, and
   end on the strongest line.
5. **"Who shaped the system?":** open on the question itself rather than clearing the throat.
6. **Nation and restitution:** apply the end-of-paragraph test and mark where the pull dies. No length target.
7. **Borrowing:** "farther away than Baotou, in time rather than space" from Earned for "The unborn" (author
   to say yes or no).
8. **Fact flag for Session 2:** the BP–Mexico comparison in "A coast" ("Measured against comparable lengths
   of damaged coastline…") isn't in the ledger. Verify it or soften it to what the sources support. It is not
   in the NYRB essay (checked).

## Structure question (author, 2026-11-10; awaiting the author's choice)

The author asked whether the zoom should run pacemaker → bigger and bigger → Baotou, then tie Baotou back to
the cellphone in the reader's pocket (ending there or elsewhere); or run Baotou → smaller and smaller → the
pacemaker, then jump: the device maker benefits from the dying the way we benefit from the villagers, and the
cellphone puts the reader among those benefiting.

Claude's observation: the current Out and back zoom wobbles. It peaks at the planet (Baotou, "A supply
chain") and then steps back down to the nation (2008) before turning to time.

- **A. As drafted:** room → household → town → coast → supply chain → nation → time → unborn → argument →
  room.
- **B. Claude's recommendation:** room → household → town → coast → nation (2008: the reader *paid*) → planet
  (Baotou: the reader *benefits*, and the cellphone collapses the farthest distance into the reader's pocket)
  → time → unborn → argument → room. It fixes the scale order, puts the bridge at the apex of the zoom, and
  puts the reader on both sides of the mechanism (honest accounting, no villain). The nation section's "alibi"
  sentence drops its supply-chain item. The close can gather both ends: the room most families will sit in,
  and the cellphone in the reader's pocket.
- **C. The author's second option:** Baotou → in → the pacemaker → back out to Baotou and the cellphone. It is
  close to Earned roadmap's spine, which the author passed over, and it ends on the reader's complicity. The
  jump needs care: the article supports an asymmetry (about $7,500 to the device maker, nothing for the
  mother's eighty-hour weeks), not "benefits from the dead," which would cut against the no-villain reading.
  Time and the unborn have no natural place in a zoom inward.

## Author, 2026-11-10: B picked; then a clarification that reopens it (discussion in progress)

- **Author picked B.** Then clarified: "I didn't pass over the spine because of the spine. I passed over
  [it] because I live on a boat and you drafted it as though everyone lives in a house and it felt foreign &
  contrived to me. The flow however was my favorite part of the other version." So Claude's objection to C
  ("you've already passed over this spine") was wrong, and the house assumption is the real lesson (see lens
  8, relationships before objects). Out and back's town section still opens on the attic; replace it.
- **Claude's synthesis (H), offered for the author's choice between B and H:** open in the room with the
  question only (the mother's request, "Please help me get Jeff's pacemaker turned off"); jump to the farthest
  place (Baotou: villages, then the cellphone); come closer case by case (coasts, Libby: the reader benefits
  at a distance); turn at 2008 (now the reader pays); Social Security (parents' benefits, children's taxes);
  arrive back in the room with the answer (what happened, the fees, the eighty hours, her own words); then
  the crowdfunding evidence as the generalization ("rooms like it are in every town"); the argument sections;
  close on the one distance that can't close, the unborn ("farther away than Baotou, in time rather than
  space"), and the bond question. It keeps Out and back's opening, Earned roadmap's closing-distance flow and
  Roadmap first's turn from benefiting to paying, with no house. Stakes rise at every step. Risk: a bigger
  restructure than a pull pass, and the opening must hold back the answer without feeling coy.
- **Page-two drop (discussion):** right after the heartbreak, "A household" changes the subject from one
  family to fundraising platforms, opens on a scale announcement and runs ~500 words through six sources with
  no person. Statistics generalize a story, so they belong after the story they generalize, and short. Under
  H the problem goes away (crowdfunding follows the completed Butler story). Under B: open on community (a
  family that can't pay asks the people who know it), give the outcomes as the punch, and keep it short.
  Either way, move Mishel–Bivens and Hacker's risk shift to the 2008 section, where the draft itself says the
  risk shift "will turn up again."
- **"Who shaped the system?" (discussion):** as drafted it asks who wrote today's rules that sent the cost
  where it went (lobbying and captured regulators: Libby, the Minerals Management Service, the 1999–2000
  deregulation, the Medicare drug-price ban). It is the book's bridge to Public Choice readers. The deep
  history the author remembers (the pharaohs and the pyramids, the Qin emperor and the Great Wall) lives in
  Ch 7 and Ch 10, where it shows the severance itself is ancient and can run on power with no distance at all.
  Claude's recommendation: keep the history there; open the section on its own best line, now buried third:
  "The ledger shows where the cost landed, but it does not show who built the chute it slid down."
- **Cross-chapter coherence flag for Session 2's critic read:** Ch 5 says the mechanism "needs only
  distance"; Ch 7 and Ch 10 say the pyramids ran on power with zero geographic distance. Check that Ch 5's
  "distance" is meant broadly, or adjust the line.

## Author, 2026-11-10 (later): Earned roadmap's stop point, and the rings shape

- **Earned roadmap's Baotou also fell flat after page one:** "the prose was a bit clunky and it didn't have
  any pacing that made me want to keep reading beyond the first page. The transition after the first scene
  had me naturally stop reading on two separate read throughs." Claude's earlier claim that Earned's opening
  "works" is withdrawn. The stop point is the turn from the scene into the roadmap list and the abstract
  thesis ("It works like this…"). **Lesson:** after an opening scene, don't halt the momentum with a roadmap
  and a thesis. Carry the reader into the next case with a question, and name the pattern later (the author's
  own first-redline direction: show, then name).
- **The author's shape:** "starting at a connection to one's family. Then community. Then country. Then world
  only to bring it back to something in your pocket showing you how it's all connected." Stakes rising toward
  the reader also appeals.
- **Claude's read: this beats both B and H as framed ("R", for rings).** The rings are the reader's circles of
  belonging, not geographic scale, so the stakes stay tied to the reader at every step ("your" family,
  community, country, world). That fixes B's falling stakes and keeps Out and back's whole opening scene. The
  pocket is the climax and a turn: the reader has paid or watched their community pay in every ring, and the
  cellphone shows they also benefit. No alibi, no villain. It hands off directly to "The pattern, named,"
  which answers "how it's all connected."
- **R in order (proposed; the author confirms):**
  1. **Family:** Butler's room, told whole (Session 1's quotes and rewrite). End on a question, not a thesis.
     Name the pattern in a sentence or two at most, and move the full statement (the author's lines "cashed
     out," "left standing," "the bill comes due") to "The pattern, named."
  2. **Community:** a family that can't pay asks its community (crowdfunding, short; nine in ten fall short;
     the thinnest networks get the least); Libby (the dust on work clothes, in kitchens, on children's laps;
     no attic); the fishing communities of the Gulf and Prince William Sound (the season stopped arriving).
  3. **Country:** 2008 ("the alibi fails"; the author's own 2008; Mishel–Bivens and Hacker's risk shift
     move here); Social Security (the compact between generations: parents' benefits, children's taxes).
  4. **World:** Baotou (villages first: illness, birth defects, farmland).
  5. **The pocket:** the cellphone in your pocket, and in mine. It's all connected.
  6. **The pattern, named,** then the argument sections ("Who shaped the system?" opening on the chute line).
  7. **Close:** the outermost ring is family again: the unborn, our descendants, "farther away than Baotou,
     in time rather than space." A brief return to the room is optional. End on the bond question.
- **Care points:** the opening needs a puzzle that the pocket answers, about the pattern, not a material
  link (don't imply rare earths in pacemakers). The refrain can name the rings. The back half follows the
  climax, so the argument sections still need the section-ending and opener fixes.
