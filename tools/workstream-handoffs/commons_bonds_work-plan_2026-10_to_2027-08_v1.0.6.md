## Top priority: the Darity package (Chapter 1 + Chapter 5 + Technical Appendix)

**His request:** the corrected TA plus **any two chapters of your choosing**. You chose Chapters 1 and 5: Chapter 1 shows the narrative voice and how the book opens; Chapter 5 is the strongest chapter and the one closest to his field. Chapter 6 is the alternative pairing for the appendix, but the appendix already carries the math, and Ch 6 needs a full rebuild first. Darity's own framing: the book isn't just the appendix. If the prose can't carry an idea smoothly, the book won't work, so the sample chapters matter as much as the math. He's also the most credible route to Duke University Press and his other publishers. Everything below serves getting these three pieces to the highest standard.

**Chapter 5 grounding test (part of the rebuild brief and the judge, author-directed 2026-10-25).** Chapter 5 climbs from a town to an ecosystem, a supply chain, a national economy, time itself and a single dying body. That's the most abstract ground in the book, at ~14k words. **Every step up in scale must stay tied to a named person, place or documented scene** from the canonical record, under the no-invented-facts rule. Don't let a step run longer than a few paragraphs of pure argument before it returns to the ground. The judge scores each candidate on this explicitly, alongside fidelity, positioning and voice, and your redline uses it as the test for whether the chapter is working.

**Why stagger rather than one package in January:** he asked for the corrected TA and said he'd take his time with it, so sending it in December starts that clock a month sooner. Chapter 1 is ready, and sending it alongside answers his point about the writing right away. Chapter 5 then arrives as the second sample: a second, well-timed contact rather than a long silence. *Alternative, if you'd rather he see everything at once:* hold the TA and Chapter 1 and send all three Jan 4–5. That's a clean choice either way. Decide by W6.

**Agent queries wait for his read.** If Darity offers an introduction to Duke University Press (or UNC, or another of his publishers), that route doesn't need an agent, and an agent query already in flight could complicate it. So Wave 1 holds until he responds to the package, up to about mid-February, and then you choose the route (see Window 3). The proposal still gets finished over break either way, because a press needs it as much as an agent does.

**On the foreword:** your instinct is right. Ask only if he raises publishers or the book's future on his own. Forewords are usually requested once a book has a home (often by the press), and a request that comes before he's read the package could make his honest critique feel transactional. His candor is the asset. In the meantime, the best thing for the relationship is to treat his comments seriously and show him what changed because of them.

**Keep other routes alive.** Don't let everything ride on one person. Agent targets and Pipeline B (UNC → Kinney, Duke → Wissoker/Berger, HUP → Tovbis, per the June editor research) stay ready, so a slow or no answer doesn't stall the book.
