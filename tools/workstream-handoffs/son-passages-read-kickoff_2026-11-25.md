## The question to answer

For each passage, and then for the set as a whole:

1. **What work does it do for the book's argument?** The book's claim is that costs are severed from the
   people who gain and land on people who cannot refuse them. Does the passage show that, in a way nothing
   else in the chapter shows? Or does it ask the reader for sympathy the argument does not need?
2. **What would be lost if it were shorter, or gone?** Try the chapter in your head three ways: as it stands;
   with the hospital scene kept and line 140 cut; with the son reduced to the single clause at line 106.
3. **Does it pull attention away?** After line 140, is the reader thinking about severed costs or about what
   happened in this family? Does the reader finish with a question the book never answers (why there is no
   contact), and does that unanswered question help or hurt?
4. **How does it read to the readers who matter first?** A scholar reading sample chapters to judge whether
   the author can write and whether the framework is serious; a university-press editor; and the reader the
   book is for, someone weighing work that will cost them time with their own family.
5. **Is there a risk beyond the page?** His son is a living, identifiable person who has not been asked. Check
   the passage against `tools/memory/feedback_named_subject_consent.md` and say whether it raises anything
   under that policy even though no name is given.

## Rules for this read

- **Give a verdict, with reasons.** He asked because he cannot judge it himself. "It depends" is not useful
  to him. If the answer differs by passage, say which stay, which shrink and which go.
- **Make the strongest case for the other side** before you settle, in a short paragraph of its own.
- **Invent nothing.** Do not write replacement prose about his son, his marriage or his feelings. If you
  recommend a cut or a move, describe it ("end the chapter at line 138"; "keep lines 116–118, drop 140"). Any
  new first-person sentence comes from him.
- **Be kind and be plain.** This is the hardest material in the book for him. Do not soften the finding, and
  do not speculate about the family.
- No panels, no repeat runs, no second opinion from a subagent. One reader, one read.

## What to hand back

One short file, `tools/audits/son-passages-read_<date>.md`: the verdict in the first three lines; then each
passage with keep, shrink or cut and one or two sentences of reasoning; the case for the other side; what you
found in the other chapters; and the consent point. Commit it and push to main as internal scaffolding. Then
tell him the verdict in the conversation in a few sentences, and stop. He decides.
