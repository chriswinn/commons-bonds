## 3. Candidate placements

### Candidate A — End of §4 (after [Chapter_10_CommonBonds__Draft.md:63](manuscript/chapters/Chapter_10_CommonBonds__Draft.md:63))

**Rationale:** §4 is the meta-reflection on model capabilities. Bidirectionality is also a capability claim.

**What's at stake:** §4 has a clean three-cannot / three-can architecture. The bidirectionality paragraph is a different *kind* of claim — it's not "what the model does for the reader who applies it" (§4's three "can" items) but "what the framework can do that this book chose not to demonstrate." Inserting here disrupts §4's symmetric architecture and dilutes its register.

**Verdict:** Plausible but architecturally weak.

### Candidate B — Between §5 (Lakota close, [:75](manuscript/chapters/Chapter_10_CommonBonds__Draft.md:75)) and §6 (Reformist open, [:79](manuscript/chapters/Chapter_10_CommonBonds__Draft.md:79)) — RECOMMENDED

**Rationale:** §5's opening explicitly distinguishes its register from §4: *"There is one more concession I owe the reader, and it sits differently from the others. The others are things the model cannot do. This one is something this book has not fully done."* The bidirectionality paragraph is also a "things this book has not fully done" claim — but at the framework-reach meta-level, generalizing the Lakota-specific concession into a structural statement about apparatus reach.

The Lakota section closes (line 75) with: *"...the absence of the mechanism's name is what permits the case to be treated as historical aberration rather than as the system's foundation."* This generalization-to-systemic-pattern primes the reader for the bidirectionality paragraph's framework-level meta-claim.

The Reformist section opens (line 79) with: *"There is one objection I have been waiting to answer until here..."* — a clean pivot that does not depend on continuity with §5 specifically. So a new mini-section between §5 and §6 does not disrupt §6's opening.

**Adjacency benefit:** the bidirectionality paragraph implicitly answers a tension §6 raises. §6 worries the framework "can be captured / softened / used to justify continued extraction." The bidirectionality paragraph (just before) tells the reader the framework's REACH is broader than what this book showed — including the backward-direction restitution work that §6 implicitly worries reformist capture might foreclose.

**What's at stake:** §5 already feels weighted with concession-work; adding a fourth Lakota-section paragraph would over-load it, but inserting as its own mini-section preserves §5's contained architecture while honoring the bidirectionality paragraph's distinct register.

**Verdict:** Recommended.

### Candidate C — As a new section before the Lakota section (between §4 and §5, after [:65](manuscript/chapters/Chapter_10_CommonBonds__Draft.md:65))

**Rationale:** A bridge section that establishes the book/framework distinction *before* the Lakota concession, so Lakota reads as the most weight-bearing instance of the bidirectionality the book chose not to fully execute.
