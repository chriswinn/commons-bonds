founded in late 1968 but the strike was Feb-Mar 1969). This is a Harper's

issue is a separate Harper's-internal matter and does NOT undermine the

## §2. WVBLA founders (Brooks / Miller / Mullins) — PARTIALLY VERIFIED

Parent session asserted three founders + early-1969 formation:
"WVBLA formed early 1969 by Charles Brooks / Arnold Miller / Woodrow
Mullins as political/organizing umbrella."

Triangulated source findings:

- **Founding date: VERIFIED with one corrigendum.** The dominant
  historical record dates WVBLA to **December 1968** in Montgomery, WV
  (not "early 1969"). One source (Manifold OEN *Accessible Appalachia*
  Ch 17) describes the formation as "culminat[ing] in early 1969 with
  the formation of the West Virginia Black Lung Association, which was
  officially introduced at a rally in Beckley." This suggests a two-stage
  founding: a December 1968 organizing meeting in Montgomery → a
  formal/public introduction at a Beckley rally in early 1969. Both
  framings appear in the literature; parent session's "early 1969" is
  defensible-but-not-canonical (most sources say December 1968).
- **Charles Brooks: VERIFIED as first president.** African-American coal
  miner from Cedar Grove. Mortgaged his home to hire former State Senator
  Paul Kaufman to lobby the WV legislature. Confirmed by multiple
  sources (UMWA Journal, WV Encyclopedia, Appalachian Voices,
  Appalachian Citizens' Law Center).
- **Arnold Miller: PARTIALLY VERIFIED.** Confirmed as an early
  organizer ("Late in 1968, Miller organized other ill miners into the
  West Virginia Black Lung Association"), but elected WVBLA *president*
  in **1970**, not 1968. Became UMW president 1972 (parent session's
  parenthetical is correct on the UMW-presidency timing). Source: WV
  Encyclopedia entry on Arnold Miller + Wikipedia + WV Culture archive.
- **Woodrow Mullins: VERIFIED as founding member.** Operated the WVBLA
  local in Gallagher; his wife participated in the movement alongside
  other miners' wives. Confirmed by Southern Spaces 2021 + Activist
  History Review.
- **Founders parent session OMITTED:** The Physicians Committee for
  Mine Health and Safety — **Dr. Isadore E. Buff (Charleston
  cardiologist), Dr. Hawey Wells, Dr. Donald L. Rasmussen** — are
  consistently named in the historical record as catalytic to the
  movement (touring coal camps; doing the medical-recognition advocacy
  that Brooks et al. organized politically). They are not *founders* of
  WVBLA per se, but they are inseparable from the formation story. Any
  inventory framing that names Brooks/Miller/Mullins but omits the
  Physicians Committee is structurally incomplete. (Not a HIGH severity
  issue for the inventory because the inventory's purpose is fact-ground
  for op-ed/essay drafting, not comprehensive movement history; flagging
  as MEDIUM.)

**Verdict §2: PARTIALLY VERIFIED.** Brooks confirmed; Mullins confirmed;
Miller's role accurate but his WVBLA *presidency* came in 1970 (not at
founding). Founding date should be **December 1968** as canonical (with
Beckley-rally formal introduction in early 1969 as auxiliary detail).
Physicians Committee (Buff/Wells/Rasmussen) is a notable omission.

## §3. WV HB1 / Gov. Arch Moore March 1969 — MOSTLY VERIFIED

Parent session asserted: "1969 wildcat strikes pressured WV HB1 signed
by Gov. Arch Moore March 1969."

Triangulated findings:

- **Strike timing: VERIFIED.** Wildcat strike began Feb 18, 1969 at
  the East Gulf Mine (Rhodell, Raleigh County) with 282 miners. By
  early March ~40,000-45,000 WV miners on strike. Black Lung Rally at
  state capitol Feb 26, 1969 (2,000 miners). Source: Zinn Education
  Project, Clio, WV Public Broadcasting.
- **Signing date: VERIFIED specifically as MARCH 11, 1969.** Multiple
  sources confirm Governor Moore signed the bill on March 11, 1969;
  miners returned to work the morning after. Parent session's "March
  1969" is correct but under-specific.
- **Governor identity: VERIFIED.** Arch A. Moore Jr., WV Gov 1969-1977
  (first term, just inaugurated Jan 1969). Confirmed by multiple sources.
- **Bill designation as "HB1": NOT INDEPENDENTLY CONFIRMED.** None of
  the WebSearch-accessible sources designate the bill as "House Bill 1"
  by that specific name. Sources describe it as "the state's first black
  lung legislation" / "WV pneumoconiosis statute" / "the bill." This
  does NOT mean parent session's "HB1" claim is wrong — it may be
  correct per WV legislative records — but it is NOT verified in this
  audit's source pool. Flagging as MEDIUM uncertainty.
- **Causal direction: VERIFIED.** Strikes did directly pressure the bill
  (Zinn + Clio + WV Public Broadcasting all describe the timeline:
  walkout → legislature initial weak bill → 40K-miner statewide strike
  → stronger bill → Moore signing → return to work).

**Verdict §3: MOSTLY VERIFIED.** Strike → bill → signing chain is
solid; March 11, 1969 + Gov. Arch Moore Jr. confirmed; the specific
"HB1" designation is unconfirmed in this audit's source pool (sources
describe the bill generically rather than by number).

## §4. Federal Coal Mine Health and Safety Act 1969 connection — VERIFIED

Parent session asserted: "1969 wildcat strikes helped force the federal
Coal Mine Health and Safety Act December 1969."

Triangulated findings:

- **Federal Act signing date: VERIFIED.** December 30, 1969, signed by
  President Richard Nixon. Confirmed by MSHA, Wikipedia, WV Public
  Broadcasting, American Presidency Project, SSA Bulletin.
- **WV strikes → federal Act causal chain: VERIFIED.** Multiple sources
  describe the WV strikes as accelerating federal action ("the 1969
  Black Lung Association's strike ... accelerated the following year"
  per Harper's framing; "By the end of the year, Congress passed the
  Coal Mine Health and Safety Act" per Clio; "In the spring of 1969,
  40,000 miners defied their union and went on strike to support the
  legislation" per multiple sources tying state-level strikes to federal
  Coal Act passage).
- **Co-causal pressures (NOT exclusively WV strikes):** The historical
  record is clear that the Farmington Mine Disaster (Nov 20, 1968 —
  Consolidation Coal Company No. 9 mine, 78 miners killed) was the other
  major catalyst, alongside Ralph Nader's federal advocacy and the
  Physicians Committee tour. Parent session's "1969 wildcat strikes
  helped force" language correctly uses "helped" rather than "caused"
  — accurate framing of the multi-causal pressure chain.

**Verdict §4: VERIFIED.** Federal Act = Dec 30, 1969, Nixon. WV strikes
were one major causal contributor among several (Farmington 1968 disaster
+ Physicians Committee + Nader). Parent session's "helped force" framing
correctly preserves the multi-causal structure.

  (a) Gary Fox, a miner who died of black lung;
  (b) John Cline, a lawyer who came to WV as a VISTA volunteer and

  The book covers the resurgent modern black-lung crisis (post-2000)

  1969 movement origins.

  the Farmington Mine Disaster, was supposed to ... virtually eliminate

  analytical core.
- **Reviews do NOT address whether Hamby supports a "BLA led the strike"
  (umbrella-causal) reading vs a "BLA + strike were parallel forces"

- **Harper's essay line 71 separately cites Hamby** for "the longer

**Verdict §5: UNVERIFIABLE without direct book access.** The parent
session's claim that Hamby specifically supports the "umbrella reading"

verifiable from review evidence.** This is exactly the kind of

## §6. Rank-and-file vs WVBLA-led precision — VERDICT REVERSED

Parent session's parenthetical asserted: "WVBLA was the organizing
umbrella; the strike actions were rank-and-file miner actions tied to
and supported by WVBLA."

Triangulated source findings on the actual strike dynamics:

- **The strike was wildcat — meaning: NOT authorized by UMW leadership
  AND launched by rank-and-file miners.** "On February 18, 1969, coal
  miners at the East Gulf Mine in West Virginia went on an unauthorized
  strike, not content with their union's efforts to recognize black lung
  disease ... Rank-and-file coal miners orchestrated a wave of wildcat
  strikes" (Zinn Education Project + WV Public Broadcasting).
- **WVBLA's role was political/organizing, not strike-calling.** WVBLA
  was formed in Dec 1968 explicitly to "prepare a bill that would
  require miners suffering from Black lung to be compensated fairly"
  (Clio). Brooks's first move was hiring Paul Kaufman to lobby the
  legislature. The strike was a parallel rank-and-file action that
  escalated in support of the legislative push WVBLA was already running.
- **But the strike-organizing was NOT clean-rank-and-file either.**
  Multiple sources describe Arnold Miller (WVBLA organizer) as
  helping "the Black Lung Association lead a three-week coal strike"
  (multiple WVBLA-leadership accounts). The reality is BOTH/AND: the
  initial East Gulf walkout was rank-and-file (282 miners; not by WVBLA
  call); WVBLA + organizing miners then channeled/amplified the
  spreading walkouts into the legislative-pressure campaign.

**Verdict §6: The parent session's parenthetical phrasing is MORE
HISTORICALLY ACCURATE than the simplified "BLA led the strike" framing
in Harper's line 37.** The historical record genuinely is parallel-
causal-with-WVBLA-amplification, not pure-WVBLA-led. The original
inventory phrasing ("West Virginia Black Lung Association and a miners'
strike forced the legislative issue" — and-conjunction, parallel
framing) was actually closer to the historical record than the Harper's-
aligned "wildcat strike led by the West Virginia Black Lung Association"
that the inventory fix installed.

**This creates a tension the audit must surface:**

- **The inventory fix aligns canonical inventory with Harper's RATIFIED
  text.** That is the parent session's primary justification and it is
  valid as a portfolio-consistency move.
- **But Harper's RATIFIED text itself is a simplification of the
  historical record.** The parent session's parenthetical correctly
  diagnoses this ("WVBLA was the organizing umbrella; the strike
  actions were rank-and-file miner actions") and preserves the nuance
  in the inventory.
- **Net effect:** The fix is acceptable BECAUSE the parenthetical
  rescues the nuance lost in the main-clause alignment. If only the
  main clause had been changed (without the parenthetical), the fix
  would be a HIGH-severity regression.

**Severity:** No finding required against the inventory-fix-as-shipped;
the parenthetical does the work. Separate finding for Harper's-side
consideration: Harper's line 37's "wildcat strike that the Black Lung
Association led" is a defensible journalistic simplification (Harper's
register tolerates this), but if Harper's gets editor-round revision,
this is a clean candidate for precision-upgrade to "wildcat strike
that miners launched and the Black Lung Association amplified into
legislative pressure" or similar. Flagging as separate downstream
consideration; NOT a Workstream B finding.
