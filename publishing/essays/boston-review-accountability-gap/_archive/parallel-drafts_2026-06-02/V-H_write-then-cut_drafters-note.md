## §1. The discipline V-H operationalizes

The author's framing in their own words:

> "I have noticed you seem to apply compression almost for the sake of compression to almost everything you write and that tends to make your points less clear. I think often you use extra sentences to say something when in reality it could have been said clearer with less words even if that means you leave some detail out, you would have to compress less often to fit within words... instead of compressing, I actually cut information out that isn't directly supporting and relevant to the subject matter and intent of the piece."

V-H operationalizes this as:

1. **Write each section fully** at natural prose pace from substrate.
2. **Identify the load-bearing ideas** for that section's contribution to the essay's spine.
3. **Cut the ideas that aren't supporting those load-bearing claims** — paragraphs, sentences, entire claims — rather than compressing them.
4. **Do not compress the load-bearing ideas that remain.** Let them sit at their natural full prose length.

The success criterion is **clarity through fewer ideas at full length**, not density through more ideas compressed.

V-E (4,858w body; result of compounded edits over V-D and LOCKED; 20 em-dashes) operates at the opposite end of the discipline — more ideas, each sometimes tightened to fit the cumulative envelope. V-H tests whether shipping fewer ideas at fuller length produces a clearer end-reader-facing piece for the Amendment-D-reweighted DIRECT-reader audience (C-2 BR reader, C-11 general policy-curious, C-12 center-right, C-13 extraction-affected, C-15 first-gen, C-16 EJ-movement).
