Dear Editors,

I am writing to submit an essay, "Commons Bonds," of roughly [SET FINAL TRIMMED COUNT] words, for your consideration.

The essay's argument is a positioning one, and I think it belongs in a conversation Noema has been hosting. Mariana Mazzucato, in *The Value of Everything*, taught us to tell value creation apart from value extraction; the essay agrees with her diagnosis and adds the instrument she stops short of, an accounting kept arrangement by arrangement of who captured the value, who absorbed the cost, and whether the cost-bearer had any way to send the bill back. It stands with the Polanyian tradition, from *The Great Transformation* through Nancy Fraser's *Cannibal Capitalism*, on the diagnosis that markets consume land, labor, and the social fabric as free inputs, and it breaks with that tradition, in the open, on the remedy: where Polanyi and Fraser would rescue the commons by lifting it out of the price system, the essay argues that pricelessness has protected nothing, and that the honest move is to put both sides of the ledger on the same page. It reads Elinor Ostrom's *Governing the Commons* as describing a commons whose users share its fate, and locates its own contribution precisely where that shared fate breaks. It answers Michael Sandel's *What Money Can't Buy* directly, crediting his diagnosis and inverting his conclusion. And it sets William Darity and A. Kirsten Mullen's *From Here to Equality* as the worked example of an honest cost-side ledger, while declining, in the first person, to fill the column that is not mine to total.

I have tried to earn each of those moves rather than assert them. The essay opens on a colleague, the most credentialed person in a building where I was the least, who could not leave his job or press for fair pay because his right to remain in the country depended on it. It carries reportage from the Chesapeake, where I grew up, with on-record testimony from Allison Colden and Chris Moore of the Chesapeake Bay Foundation on a fishery in collapse, and it ends in my own family, at the limit where even an honest ledger cannot undo the damage it records. Memoir, theory, and reportage are meant to hold each other up.

A note on the writing partnership appears below, offered in keeping with the essay's own argument about honest accounting.

Chris Winn is a nursing student at Virginia Peninsula Community College (Hampton, Virginia) and former healthcare-IT executive whose career spanned the National Institutes of Health during the Human Genome Project, federal research and consulting, and the CEO seat of a healthcare-IT firm. His forthcoming book *Commons Bonds* develops a formal framework for pricing the intergenerational costs that resource extraction systematically severs from the populations that bear them. He lives aboard a sailboat in Hampton.

Thank you for your time and your reading.

With appreciation,
Chris Winn
