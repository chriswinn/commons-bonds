## The rule the author set (2026-11-15)

A comp earns its place by fitting the book. Its age does not matter. A recent book that does not fit is
worth nothing. A book the proposal argues against, or a very large bestseller, is a positioning line and
not a comp.

### Include as comps, whatever their age

| Work | What the appendix says of it | Why it belongs in §02 |
|---|---|---|
| Darity and Mullen, *From Here to Equality* (UNC Press, 2020; 2nd ed. 2022) | The method for pricing harm already done; restitution as the act of redress (contribution table; §5.4) | The backward half of the book rests on it. It is also the closest model of a book that diagnoses, prices and designs. Already a lead. |
| Ostrom, *Governing the Commons* (Cambridge, 1990) | Governance by those who share a resource's fate; the framework prices the cases where the people who bear the cost have no hand in the rules (§14.5) | Any reader who sees "commons" in the title will think of Ostrom first. The proposal should say in one paragraph where the book stands beside hers. **Not in §02 today. Add.** |

| Patel and Moore, *A History of the World in Seven Cheap Things* (UC Press, 2017) | In the bibliography | Already a lead. "Cheap" in their sense is the unpaid cost in this book's sense. |

Pistor, *The Code of Capital* (2019), Christophers, *The Price Is Wrong* (2024) and Susskind, *Growth: A
Reckoning* (2024) are leads in §02 and are used in the overview, but none of the three is cited in the
appendix. They stay on the list on the strength of the chapters and the overview.

### Name in one positioning paragraph, not as comps

These are papers, reports or classics. A proposal reader does not weigh them as comparable books, and a
university-press reviewer will want to see them credited.

- Costanza and Perrings 1990, the assurance bond. The appendix calls it the closest prior instrument (§14.8).
- Hotelling 1931, Hartwick 1977, Solow 1974, Weitzman 2001, Pigou 1920, El Serafy 1989.
- Harvey, *The New Imperialism* (2003): "Harvey diagnoses the mechanism; RCV measures its size" (§14.7).
- Daly on strong sustainability (§14.6); Stern, *The Economics of Climate Change* (2007).
- Rawls 1971, Parfit 1984 and Sen 1999 for the moral grounding (§5.4, §14.4).
- TEEB 2010 and 2018; Epstein et al. 2011; Muller, Mendelsohn and Nordhaus 2011.

### In the appendix bibliography, left out on purpose

Desmond, *Evicted* (2016) and Acemoglu and Robinson, *Why Nations Fail* (2012): too large to be believed
as comps. Hickel, *Less Is More* (2020): on the bench in §02 already; the book does not argue degrowth.
Mian and Sufi, *House of Debt* (2014): supports one case in Chapter 5, not the book.

### A positioning line, not a comp

Klein and Thompson, *Abundance* (Avid Reader Press, March 2025), spent months on the *New York Times*
nonfiction list. It is far too large to be a comp. It matters because "abundance" is now a word editors
have in their heads, and this book's claim is that abundance is what hides a cost. **Author's ruling, 2026-11-15: yes, as a marked
candidate sentence in the overview print copy, pending his read of the book.** Start from the contrast of
questions (why building costs so much and takes so long; what was left off the bill when things were built
cheaply). No statement about the authors' motives.

### Looked for and not found

A 2025 or 2026 book from a trade or university press whose main subject is who pays the unpriced cost of
extraction, with a figure attached. Two searches found none. That absence is itself a line for the
proposal's market section, if a fuller search confirms it.

## Suggested shape for §02 (the author decides)

- **University-press version, eight entries:** Darity and Mullen; Ostrom; Pistor; Mazzucato; Patel and
  Moore; Christophers; Dasgupta; Davies. Then one positioning paragraph crediting the papers and classics.
- **Trade version, five or six:** lead with the recent ones (Christophers, Davies, Dasgupta, Riofrancos,
  Susskind), then Darity and Mullen and Mazzucato as the established anchors.
- Coyle, Jones and Romeo are the reserves. Hochschild only if the trade version wants a narrative comp.
