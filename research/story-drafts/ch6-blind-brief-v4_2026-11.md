## 1. What changed since v3

1. **The title stays, with a gloss, and the "three converge" story is gone for good** (author, TA decision 1, 2026-11-11). Two ways count what is owed; one counts what was paid. Counting what was paid is a real part of balancing the books: the difference between owed and paid is the accountability gap. Use no "triangulation" and no "convergence" anywhere, for the inner or the outer set. ## 4. Positioning (verified 2026-06-24; apply exactly)

Source: `tools/workstream-handoffs/verified-positioning-findings_2026-06-24.md`. Read its "Bottom line" paragraph and nothing else from it.

- **Ostrom.** Her design principles govern commons held by a bounded group of appropriators who deal with each other repeatedly and substantially share the resource's fate; her own early groundwater work sits inside that domain. The framework's cases are the ones where that shared fate is missing: the party taking the value stands outside the group, or the cost-bearer is present but dominated. **Never write "coequal" or "equals."** Never say Ostrom is blind to power, and never say unequal stakes break her cooperation. For the critique that the principles can conceal power, cite Mudliar and Koontz (2021) and Harvey.
- **Who diagnosed the asymmetry first.** Kapp (1950), and political ecology and environmental justice (Martínez-Alier; Bullard). Credit them.
- **Polanyi and Fraser** diagnosed and refused to price, on principle. The book does cost accounting to force a reckoning; it does not make an asset. Never say they would endorse pricing. (Ch 5 carries this; one sentence here at most.)
- **What is added.** A price for the residual commons value of a single extraction event, with a reserve posted in advance against that figure. Not the idea of pricing natural capital, and not the idea of a bond.
- **Closest prior art, each named and distinguished in a sentence:** Costanza and Perrings (1990), a bond posted in advance but sized to worst-case total damage; natural resource damage assessment, which is after the fact; TEEB and true-cost accounting, which evaluate at the scale of a sector; Hotelling's user cost and El Serafy's, which price the owner's forgone rent, not a third party's loss; Hartwick and Dasgupta, a savings rule and national accounts; Epstein et al. (2011) and Muller, Mendelsohn and Nordhaus (2011), full-cost and damage accounting for coal and for industries. Confirm each citation in the bibliography before use.
- **Pigou.** Concede that he priced the externality and that the move to internalize a cost through a price is his.
- **Darity and Mullen.** "The method is theirs; the framework adopts it." Quote only published wording. The longevity-gap passage: check the figure against the published paper (v3 correction flag 1) or leave it out. Nothing from the May 2026 call, in any form.
