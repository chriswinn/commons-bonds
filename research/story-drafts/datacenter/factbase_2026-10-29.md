## Candidate "strongest facts" (verify these first)

1. **JLARC:** NoVA = 13% of world and 25% of the Americas' data-center capacity (§2/§1).
2. **Dominion 10-K:** data centers = 28% of Dominion Virginia electricity sales in 2025, up from 21% in 2022 (§1).
3. **JLARC:** the typical residential bill rises +$14 to +$37/month by 2040 for generation and transmission alone, excluding distribution (§2).
4. **Maryland OPC:** Marylanders face $1.6B over 10 years for transmission built mainly for Virginia data centers (§4). This is the cross-border externality, and it fits the book's commons argument closely.
5. **Duration mismatch:** servers are depreciated over ~5–6 years, while the transmission built for them is engineered for 40–60 years. Virginia's GS-5 answers with 14-year contracts and 85% minimums: a partial bond-like instrument, but no decommissioning security (§3, §11, §12). The pairing is the analytic point; each half needs a primary read.

Runner-up: Loudoun's data centers make up 38% of general-fund revenue (§8), the strongest benefits-side figure.
