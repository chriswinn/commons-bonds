# CBO household income thresholds, 2017 and 2018 (read 2026-11-24)

**Status:** internal verification note. Closes library-list item H1 and feeds Chapter 1 claims-check item 4
(line 126, "the top one to two percent of household incomes in the United States for those years").
**Source:** Congressional Budget Office, supplemental data for *The Distribution of Household Income, 2018*
(August 2021), www.cbo.gov/publication/57061, file `57061-Data.xlsx`, **Table 2, "Income Group Minimums, by
Household Size, 1979 to 2018."** The author downloaded the file and supplied it; the session read it. The file
is not committed.

**What the table measures (the file's own notes).** "2018 Dollars." "Income groups are created by ranking
households by income before transfers and taxes, adjusted for household size." "Income before transfers and
taxes consists of market income plus social insurance benefits." "To compute the minimum income for a
household larger than four people, multiply the one-person minimum income by the square root of the household
size."

**Lowest income that puts a household in the group, in 2018 dollars:**

| Household size | Year | 96th to 99th percentiles | Top 1 percent |
|---|---|---|---|
| One person | 2017 | $183,000 | $418,100 |
| One person | 2018 | $186,800 | $428,900 |
| Two people | 2017 | $258,800 | $591,300 |
| Two people | 2018 | $264,100 | $606,500 |
| Three people | 2017 | $317,000 | $724,200 |
| Three people | 2018 | $323,500 | $742,800 |
| Four people | 2017 | $366,000 | $836,300 |
| Four people | 2018 | $373,500 | $857,700 |

**What this does and does not settle.**
- The table has no line for "the top 2 percent." Its nearest group is the 96th to 99th percentiles, the top
  5 percent less the top 1. A claim of "top one to two percent" can be tested against the top-1-percent line
  only; between that line and the 96th-percentile line the table cannot say where the 98th falls.
- The threshold depends on household size. The author has to choose the row that matches his household in
  each year.
- The 2017 figures are stated in 2018 dollars, so a 2017 income in that year's dollars should be raised by
  inflation (about 2 percent) before comparing.
- The author's own figures are not recorded here or anywhere in the repository. He makes the comparison.
