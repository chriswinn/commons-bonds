# Library list re-test: what was read, in the sources' words (2026-11-15)

**Status:** internal verification note. Written by the session that re-tested the Chapter 5 and Chapter 1
items on `tools/back-matter/library-list.md` and worked its Part 5 queue. Every quotation below was read in
the document named, not in a search result. No chapter, appendix, ledger or working-bibliography text was
changed; the Chapter 5 and Chapter 1 claims-check reports were written by other sessions and are not edited
here. Each entry says which row it bears on.

## Pages the list asked the author to open at home, now read

- **H6. Financial Crisis Inquiry Commission, *The Financial Crisis Inquiry Report* (official edition, U.S.
  Government Publishing Office, 2011), "Conclusions of the Financial Crisis Inquiry Commission."** Read from
  govinfo.gov (`GPO-FCIC.pdf`). "We conclude this financial crisis was avoidable. The crisis was the result
  of human action and inaction, not of Mother Nature or computer models gone haywire." The other headed
  conclusions: "widespread failures in financial regulation and supervision"; "dramatic failures of corporate
  governance and risk management"; "a combination of excessive borrowing, risky investments, and lack of
  transparency"; "the government was ill prepared"; "a systemic breakdown in accountability and ethics";
  "collapsing mortgage-lending standards and the mortgage securitization pipeline"; "over-the-counter
  derivatives contributed significantly"; "the failures of credit rating agencies were essential cogs."
  The conclusions speak of complexity and opacity. A text search of them finds no statement that the
  complexity was deliberate. **Bears on:** Ch 5 row 92 (confirmed word for word) and item 13 / row 84 ("The
  complexity was no accident": still no source); TA §6.7.
- **H7. Exxon Valdez Oil Spill Trustee Council, "Pacific Herring" (status of restoration page).** Read at
  evostc.state.ak.us. "four years after the Spill a dramatic collapse of the fishery occurred, and the herring
  population has never rebounded." "The herring fishery in the Sound has been closed for 19 of the 25 years
  since the Spill." "Pacific herring have not yet met their recovery objectives and are considered not
  recovering from the effects of the Spill." The page also says: "Other factors may have contributed to the
  crash of 1993," naming disease, poor nutrition and predation, and "it is not likely that lingering oil is
  directly affecting spawning adults, eggs or larvae." The page's own arithmetic ("19 of the 25 years") dates
  its text to about 2014. **Bears on:** Ch 5 item 8.
- **H4. The 2012 criminal plea.** The Justice Department's page answers a session with a browser check, which
  was not worked around. The Environmental Protection Agency's enforcement page for the spill, read at
  epa.gov, lists: "November 15, 2012: $4 billion criminal plea agreement with BP Exploration & Production."
  **Bears on:** Ch 5 line 56; TA §11.2.
- **H2. Social Security, 2026 figures.** ssa.gov refuses sessions by every route. Congressional Research
  Service, *Social Security: Trust Funds, Financing, and the Projected Shortfall*, R49500, November 2, 2026,
  read at everycrsreport.com (first 100,000 characters): "the Social Security trustees project that the OASI
  trust fund will become depleted (i.e., insolvent) in 2032"; "ongoing revenues are expected to pay
  approximately 78% of scheduled benefits"; "The trustees project that the combined Social Security trust funds
  would become depleted in 2034"; "Starting in 2021, the Social Security program has been operating annual
  deficits." **Bears on:** Ch 5 item 4. This is the research service reporting the trustees' figures, not the
  trustees' report itself.

## Library items, now read or answered

- **L15. Kapp, *The Social Costs of Private Enterprise*, preface to the 1950 edition** (dated "Rockfall,
  Connecticut, October 1948"; it thanks Harvard University Press), read as a page scan posted by the Kapp
  foundation (kwilliam-kapp.de, "Introductions"), reprinted in a later edition at pp. xxix–xxxii: "The main
  purpose of this book is to present a detailed study of the manner in which private enterprise under
  conditions of unregulated competition tends to give rise to social costs which are not accounted for in
  entrepreneurial outlays but instead are shifted to and borne by third persons and the community as a whole."
  **Bears on:** Ch 5 line 170 (supported in his words). The 1950 title and copyright pages were not seen.
- **L10. Panjwani and Xiong, "The causes and consequences of medical crowdfunding," *Journal of Economic
  Behavior & Organization* 205 (2023): 648–667 (DOI 10.1016/j.jebo.2022.11.017).** Abstract read at
  econpapers.repec.org; the article and its SSRN preprint refuse sessions. "GoFundMe campaigns organized by
  individuals with distinctively African-American or Hispanic surnames and first-names are significantly less
  likely to meet their fundraising target and raise less funds overall than those organized by individuals
  with neutral or distinctively White names." **Bears on:** Ch 5 item 10 (the surname sentence is supported at
  the level of the published abstract).
- **L19. The two Baotou papers exist.** Crossref records: Zhou and Ge, "Estimating the environmental cost of
  mixed rare earth production with willingness to pay: A case study in Baotou, China," *The Extractive
  Industries and Society* 8(1) (2021): 340–354 (DOI 10.1016/j.exis.2020.11.010). Zhang, Zhang, Peng, Feng,
  Fang and Chen, "Allocating environmental costs of China's rare earth production to global consumption,"
  *Science of the Total Environment* 831 (2022): 154934 (DOI 10.1016/j.scitotenv.2022.154934; authors and
  volume from a search result, not Crossref). Neither was read: the publisher refuses sessions. The second
  paper's summary is about who consumes the output ($4.8 billion of cost attributed to foreign consumption in
  2010, $5.4 billion in 2015), which is not "how much waste the industry makes." **Bears on:** Ch 5 line 138.

## The untried queue (Part 5): read

- **Loewenstein, O'Donoghue and Rabin (2003)**, read (first pages): "People exaggerate the degree to which
  their future tastes will resemble their current tastes." **Bears on:** TA Definition 1.10 (supported in the
  paper's words; was abstract-level).
- **Naik, Lewin, Young, Dearwent and Lee, "Mortality from asbestos-associated disease in Libby, Montana
  1979–2011," *Journal of Exposure Science and Environmental Epidemiology* 27 (2017): 207 ff.**, read in part:
  the study "reports on asbestos-related mortality in Libby over 33 years (1979–2011)"; its table of decedents
  totals 694 (407 male, 287 female). **Bears on:** TA §11.3 (supported; and the title the appendix's §18 entry
  lacks). Note the count runs from 1979, so "through 2011" is "from 1979 to 2011."
- **Wilberg, Livings, Barkman, Morris and Robinson, "Overfishing, disease, habitat loss, and potential
  extirpation of oysters in upper Chesapeake Bay," *Marine Ecology Progress Series* 436 (2011): 131–144**, read
  in part: "oyster abundance declined 99.7%"; the abstract attributes the decline to "overfishing, disease,
  and habitat loss" and says "Fishing and disease have had substantial negative impacts on the population."
  **Bears on:** TA §11.12 (the 99.7 percent, and "treat overfishing, disease and habitat loss together": both
  supported).
- **IPCC, Sixth Assessment Report, Working Group III, Chapter 12** (official PDF, ipcc.ch), read in part:
  the cost of direct air capture is given as "USD100–300 (full range: USD84–386) tCO2–1"; "Fuss et al. (2018)
  suggest a cost range of USD600–1000 tCO2–1 for first-of-a-kind plants, and USD100–300 tCO2–1 as experience
  accumulates." **Bears on:** TA §11.9 and §3.3: the $100–300 band is supported, and so, from the same page,
  is the appendix's $600–1,000 band for first plants. The appendix's ">$1,000/ton in pessimistic" was not
  found by text search.
- **IEA, *Net Zero by 2050* (2021)** (official PDF), searched in full: no cost per ton for direct air capture
  was found in the text ("130" and "330" occur only as carbon prices and land areas). The appendix's
  "$130–$330/ton CO₂" may be in a figure, or in a different IEA publication. **Bears on:** TA §11.9: not
  confirmed.
- **Realmonte et al. (2019)**, *Nature Communications* (official PDF), read in part: "estimates in the
  literature ranging from 100 to 1000 $/tCO2." The appendix's "$100–$600/ton CO₂ at multi-gigaton scale" was
  not found as such. **Bears on:** TA §11.9: not confirmed.
