## What is the book's inference, not a source's finding

- "The employer still owned the house, the store and the currency in between." A summary of S1 and S2.
- "A wage paid that way does less to build a town … because part of it goes straight back to the company that paid it." The chapter's reasoning, resting on Athey's "cycle the value … back to the company." No source read measures the effect on a town's growth. The author may want it softened or cut.

## Cautions for the redline

1. The drafts say "some miners were handed" such an envelope; Athey says "often." The drafts are the weaker claim.
2. Athey is described only as "writing in the *West Virginia Encyclopedia*," because his profession was not confirmed from a source. Fishback is called "the economist"; his university listing (University of Arizona) carries the article.
3. Fishback's article was read in abstract only. Before the chapter goes to an outside reader, read the article itself, or keep the wording to what the abstract says (as the drafts do).
4. S1 also reports "vicious incidents of debt peonage in the state prior to 1917" tied to transported workers. Not used: it would need its own source read.
5. S3 says U.S. Steel's successor "ceased all its operations in Gary in 1984." The ledger (J5) keeps "across the 1980s" until a primary source fixes the year. The drafts keep the ledger's wording.
6. If the passage survives the redline, the figures (80 percent, 10 percent, 25 percent, 10 to 30 percent, 15,000, a dozen) get a ledger row and a gate pattern in the promotion commit.
