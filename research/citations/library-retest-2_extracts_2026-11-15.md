## Pistor, *The Code of Capital* (Princeton University Press, 2019), chapter 1, "Empire of Law"

Read from the publisher-marked sample chapter at wid.world (the extract carries no page numbers).

- "The most important ones are contract law, property rights, collateral law, trust, corporate, and bankruptcy
  law. These are the modules from which capital is coded."
- "They bestow important attributes on assets and thereby privilege its holder: Priority, which ranks competing
  claims to the same assets; durability, which extends priority claims in time; universality, which extends
  them in space; and convertibility, which operates as an insurance device that allows holders to convert
  their private credit claims into state money on demand."
- **Bears on:** Chapter 5 line 48 (the chapter's list of modules drops contract, which she names first, and
  adds choice of jurisdiction, which is not on her list); Chapter 5 line 200; decisions log 001. A §14 entry
  on Pistor for the appendix can now be written from her own text.

## Naik, Lewin, Young, Dearwent and Lee, "Mortality from asbestos-associated disease in Libby, Montana 1979–2011," *Journal of Exposure Science & Environmental Epidemiology* 27, no. 2 (2017): 207–213

Read in full (Europe PMC, PMC5318660).

- "Six hundred and ninety-four decedents were identified as having at least one asbestos-related cause of death
  and residing in our study area boundary."
- The causes counted include lung cancer and chronic obstructive pulmonary disease as well as asbestosis and
  mesothelioma. The paper reports how many such deaths would be expected in any population of that size: for
  chronic obstructive pulmonary disease as underlying cause, 187 observed against 155.68 expected; for
  asbestosis, 59 observed against 0.58 expected.
- **Bears on:** TA §11.3 and §9.5 ("Deaths from asbestos-related disease: 694 documented … about $5.1–7.6
  billion"); Chapter 5 line 40. **The 694 is the paper's count, so the number is supported. The use made of it
  says more than the paper:** these are deaths from causes that asbestos can produce, many of which would have
  occurred without the mine. Multiplying all 694 by a value of life overstates the mortality head. The excess
  over expected deaths, which the paper gives cause by cause, is the count that would stand.

## Epstein et al., "Full cost accounting for the life cycle of coal," *Annals of the New York Academy of Sciences* 1219 (2011): 73–98

Abstract and results table read (a copy on an aggregator site, found by search).

- Abstract: "costing the U.S. public a third to over one-half of a trillion dollars annually."
- Results: "$345.3 billion (range: $175.2 bn to $523.3 bn)."
- **Bears on:** TA front table, §11.1, §14.8. The appendix's words match the abstract. The paper's own low
  estimate, $175 billion, is below "a third of a trillion."

- **Bears on:** TA §11.1, §14.8. Supported as worded.

## GAO-18-351, *Black Lung Benefits Program: Options for Improving Trust Fund Finances* (May 30, 2018)

Summary page read with the fetch tool.

- The fund "has borrowed almost every year since 1979, its first complete fiscal year." "about $6.5 billion
  was forgiven, according to Department of Labor (DOL) data." Borrowing "may exceed $15 billion by 2050."
  "the costs associated with forgiving Trust Fund interest or debt would be paid by taxpayers."
- **Bears on:** TA §9.6, §5.5, §11.6; Chapter 5 item 5. Two government documents differ: this one says about
  $6.5 billion was forgiven; the Labor Department's FY 2026 budget document says $6.4 billion was forgiven and
  $6.5 billion refinanced.

## Realmonte et al. (2019), one detail beyond the other session's note

"estimates in the literature ranging from 100 to 1000 $/tCO2"; the cost cases the paper uses are about 300,
180 and 200 dollars per ton, with a long-run floor of 50. TA §11.9's "$100–$600/ton CO₂ at multi-gigaton
scale" was not found in it.

## A slip in the bibliography

§10 gives "Resurgence of a Debilitating and Entirely Preventable Respiratory Disease among Working Coal
Miners" as *American Journal of Respiratory and Critical Care Medicine* 197, no. 12 (2018): 1500–1501. Europe
PMC indexes a letter with that title in 2014 (PMC4214114, DOI 10.1164/rccm.201407-1286le). The entry appears to
join one letter's title to another's volume and pages. Not corrected here.
